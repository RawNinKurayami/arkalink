Chapter 1 ·

Devil Fruits

A single bite can change a pirate's destiny.

Some become rubber, some transform into legendary creatures, and others gain the power of a natural element. No two Fruits are identical, but they all share one characteristic: they grant extraordinary abilities in exchange for a permanent vulnerability to the sea.

In Grand Line Chronicles, Devil Fruits are customisable powers. The player defines their concept together with the Game Master, while the Fruit Die, Talents and Technique Builder govern their growth.

The Fruit does not replace the character's Combat Style. It adds to their abilities, allowing them to develop a unique way of fighting.

1.1 · Obtaining a Devil Fruit

Possessing a Devil Fruit is optional and requires the Game Master's approval.

The character can start the campaign with a Fruit or obtain one during the adventure.

When they acquire the power, the player must define its fundamental characteristics.

CharacteristicDescription
NameThe Fruit's name.
TypeParamecia, Logia or Zoan.
DescriptionThe nature of the power and its possibilities.
Fruit DieThe Grade of mastery over the power.
TraitThe fundamental ability associated with the type.
SignatureAn effect that activates under the specified conditions.
TalentsAbilities acquired through growth.

The Fruit does not have to focus on combat.

An apparently modest power can prove useful in exploration, navigation, crafting objects or social interactions.

Its effectiveness depends on the applications the character develops.

1.2 · The three Fruit types

Devil Fruits belong to three categories.

Paramecia

The unique power

Grants an extraordinary property to the body, allows a substance to be produced, or modifies matter and the environment.

Logia

The element

Allows the user to become and control a natural element, gaining its properties.

Zoan

The transformation

Allows the user to assume an animal form and a hybrid form, modifying the body and physical abilities.

Each type has its own Trait, Signature and Talent Tree.

Mythical and Ancient Fruits belong to the Zoan category, but can gain special characteristics through the abilities provided by their Tree.

1.3 · Weaknesses and interactions

Devil Fruit users share two fundamental weaknesses: the sea and Agalmatolite, known as Seastone.

The sea

When a user falls into water and remains submerged, they lose all their strength, cannot swim and become helpless. They cannot take main Actions, Bonus Actions or Reactions, or make voluntary Movement. They need to be rescued.

A Fish-Man or Merman can continue breathing underwater and does not drown, but still loses the ability to swim and remains helpless on the seabed until removed from the water.

Agalmatolite · Seastone

Contact with Seastone deprives the user of their actions, just like immersion. Contact during an opponent's turn causes them to lose the actions of their next turn, including their main Action, Bonus Action and Reactions. If contact continues, the character keeps losing all actions and cannot move voluntarily until freed.

A single contact does not impose permanent paralysis: once the loss of the next turn has elapsed, if the character is no longer touching Seastone they can act again. Continuous contact renews the condition.

Haki and interaction with Fruits

Haki is not a weakness equivalent to the sea or Seastone. Armament allows the body of a Logia to be struck despite ordinary intangibility, according to the Colour's rules, but does not automatically remove actions or indiscriminately cancel all the Fruit's properties or powers.

1.4 · The Fruit Die

The Fruit Die represents the character's level of mastery.

It does not determine which power they possess, but how well they can control it.

An inexperienced user might produce their element without being able to shape it precisely. An expert could instead use it in completely different ways.

Progression follows this scale:

DieMastery
d4The power has manifested.
d6The character is beginning to understand it.
d8Reliable control.
d10Advanced mastery.
d12Expert mastery.
d20The pinnacle of the power, required for Awakening.

The Fruit Die normally starts at d4.

1.4.1 · How it grows

The Fruit Die is not increased by spending Ability Points.

The Game Master determines its advancement when the character's growth warrants it.

An improvement can be associated with training, discovering how the Fruit works, a significant defeat or the end of a story arc.

The die should not increase automatically after every combat.

1.4.2 · What it determines

The Fruit Die serves two functions.

It limits Fruit Techniques: no Technique using the Fruit as its Source can exceed its Grade.

It unlocks Fruit Talents: each band of the Tree requires a minimum level of mastery.

Talent progression

d4–d6 · The Gift
The character explores the power's initial possibilities.

d8–d10 · Control
The Fruit becomes more versatile and efficient.

d12 · Mastery
The character develops advanced applications.

d20 · Awakening
It becomes possible to achieve the highest manifestation of the power.

1.5 · Building Fruit Techniques

Fruit Techniques use the Builder in Chapter 7, but the Fruit is a special Source. Effects must be consistent with the Power’s Core and the Identity Sheet. When the Fruit's nature allows it, the GM can authorise effects, Areas, Ranges or Persistent Zones outside a Style's ordinary catalogue. Authorisation does not remove Grade, slot or ST costs: a Fruit must actually be capable of producing the result.

To create one, the player selects Fruit as the Source and defines the Attribute, Form, Grade and effects.

The Technique must meet three fundamental conditions:

Its Grade cannot exceed the Die of the Attribute used.

Its Grade cannot exceed the Fruit Die.

The chosen effects must be consistent with the power's nature and the Builder's rules.

The Game Master checks the Technique's narrative compatibility.

A Fruit capable of producing wax can create barriers and structures. It cannot freely produce lightning just because the Catalogue contains an effect suitable for an electrical attack.

d4 and d6 Techniques

d4 and d6 Techniques have 0 Effect slots and cannot select effects that occupy slots, unless an ability expressly grants an exception.

Effects that occupy slots normally become available from Grade d8, subject to their requirements. Templates authorised by the Source do not occupy slots, but retain their own minimum Grade and cost (§7.9).

This limit concerns constructed Techniques. It does not remove the Fruit's natural Trait or the abilities of Talents already possessed.

Costs

Fruit Techniques pay the Stamina required by their effects.

Possessing a Fruit does not automatically make every Technique derived from it free.

Specific Talents can reduce or eliminate particular costs.

When an effect included in a Fruit Technique applies a Status that allows a Save, the Threshold is determined by the Technique's Grade, not directly by the Fruit Die (§1.4 of the Game Manual).

1.6 · Fruit Talents

Each Fruit type has a dedicated Tree.

Talents are acquired through Upgrades, choosing those available based on the Fruit Die and meeting any prerequisites.

Reaching a Grade unlocks access to Talents, but does not automatically grant all those in the band.

Talents can represent passive abilities, voluntary activations, Reactions or specific exceptions.

In the V2 system, a Talent requiring voluntary activation normally uses the Bonus Action. When its description specifies a Reaction, free activation or another explicit procedure, that rule takes precedence.

Passive abilities apply automatically when their conditions are met.

Chapter 2 ·

Defining a Devil Fruit

Defining a Devil Fruit

Choosing Paramecia, Logia or Zoan is not enough to determine how a Devil Fruit works.

Two Paramecia can have completely different possibilities. Two Logia can interact with the environment in different ways because of their elements' nature. Two Zoan can have radically different anatomies, forms of movement and natural abilities.

For this reason, when a Devil Fruit is acquired, the player and Game Master must complete its Identity Sheet together.

This sheet establishes what the Fruit can normally do, what it cannot do and which aspects can later be developed through Techniques and Talents.

The Identity Sheet does not automatically grant numerical bonuses.

It defines the power's natural possibilities.

Techniques and Talents determine advanced mechanical effects instead.

2.1 · Fruit Identity Sheet

Every Fruit must define at least the following elements.

FieldWhat it establishes
NameThe Fruit's name.
TypeParamecia, Logia or Zoan.
Power’s CoreA sentence describing exactly what the Fruit allows the user to do.
Matter / Phenomenon / CreatureWhat the power actually represents.
Initial applicationsWhich families of use are available from acquisition.
Origin of the powerWhere it can normally manifest from.
Valid targetsThe user, creatures, objects, the environment, existing matter or other categories.
Ordinary reachThe distance over which the power can normally be controlled.
Ordinary quantityHow much matter, surface area or how many elements can be controlled without a specific Technique.
Ordinary durationHow long what is created or modified normally lasts.
Control precisionHow finely the power can be manipulated.
Interaction with living beingsWhether and how the power can directly modify a living body.
PermanenceWhat happens to creations or modifications when control ends.
Structural limitationsWhat the Fruit cannot do or the conditions required to use it.
Natural BacklashAny consequences the power causes in the user's body.

All these elements must be established when the Fruit is introduced or before they become relevant during play.

A definition should not be:

“I can control wax.”

It should be something like:

“I can produce wax from my body and shape what I have produced. I can control it within my normal reach, create simple objects and let it solidify. I cannot control any wax that exists in the world, and I cannot directly modify the body of a living creature.”

This way, the player and Game Master already know what naturally belongs to the Fruit and what requires further development.

2.2 · Narrative possibilities and mechanical effects

The Identity Sheet grants narrative permissions, not free bonuses.

A power can allow something without automatically turning that possibility into a numerical advantage.

A character capable of creating wax can make a candle without building a Technique.

They can plug a small hole or leave a wax trail on the ground if there is no real uncertainty.

But creating a barrier capable of blocking an attack, immobilising an enemy or covering an entire zone produces a significant mechanical effect and must be represented by a Technique, a Talent or another appropriate rule.

The fundamental question is:

does this application simply describe what the power can do, or is it producing a mechanical advantage?

If it produces damage, Statuses, forced Displacement, protection, modifiers, Zones, additional attacks or other mechanical benefits, a rule normally comes into play.

2.3 · Initial applications

A Fruit does not automatically grant every theoretically imaginable application of its concept.

Its initial Applications must be defined at creation.

A Paramecia could, for example, begin by allowing the user to:

  • produce a substance;
  • or modify their own body;
  • or manipulate an existing material.

Possessing one of these abilities does not automatically grant the others.

If a Fruit initially modifies the user's body, that does not necessarily mean it can also modify other people's bodies.

If it produces a substance, that does not necessarily mean it can control all substances of the same type already present in the environment.

If it alters objects, that does not automatically mean it can alter living creatures.

New families of applications can be obtained through Talents such as Versatile Power and Second Nature.

2.4 · Ordinary reach

Every Fruit must have an agreed Ordinary reach.

It can be:

Personal
The power acts only on the user.

Touch
The user must touch whatever is affected.

Defined distance
The Fruit can act within a specified number of metres.

Visible source
The power can be exerted on a valid source the character can see.

Ordinary reach defines the Fruit's normal control.

It does not replace a Technique's Range when the Fruit Sheet authorises a ranged construction.

An offensive Technique, special Area or Range granted by the Fruit continues to follow the Builder and the Source's specific conditions.

2.5 · Quantity and scale

The Game Master should not use vague terms such as:

“a lot”, “a large quantity”, “as much as they want”.

The Sheet must provide at least an understandable reference.

For example:

“They can normally manipulate enough to fill a bucket.”

“They can alter a single human-sized object.”

“They can simultaneously control the matter present in an area the size of a room.”

The ordinary quantity is not necessarily an absolute limit.

Techniques, Talents, the Fruit Die and Awakening can allow much greater applications.

It simply establishes how the power normally works.

2.6 · Duration and permanence

The Sheet must specify what happens when the user stops controlling the power.

A generated substance might:

  • disappear;
  • remain until the end of the scene;
  • remain until destroyed;
  • or become real, permanent matter.

These possibilities are not equivalent.

Permanently creating tonnes of material has very different consequences for the world from creating a substance that disappears after a few minutes.

If the Fruit can create permanent matter, this property must be explicitly recorded in the Identity Sheet.

Narrative permanence does not replace the rules for Persistent Zones when what remains produces mechanical effects during a battle. Zones are special Source options: the power must actually leave matter, an element or a persistent condition in the environment, and the Sheet must authorise this use.

2.7 · Living creatures

Directly affecting a living creature is an important property and must be declared.

A power that modifies objects cannot automatically be applied to an enemy's body.

The Sheet must state whether the Fruit can act:

  • only on the user;
  • on willing creatures;
  • on any creature;
  • or cannot directly alter living beings.

When the application produces a mechanical effect on a hostile target, normal resolution through Techniques, Saves or other appropriate rules remains necessary.

The mere fact that the power could theoretically affect the body does not allow Defence, HP or combat conditions to be ignored.

2.8 · Structural limitations and Backlash

These two elements must be distinguished.

Structural limitation

A Structural limitation defines how the power works.

Examples:

  • must touch the target;
  • must see the source;
  • can only control matter personally produced;
  • does not work on living beings;
  • can maintain only one complex creation;
  • requires the element to be present;
  • cannot exceed a specified quantity.

A Structural limitation is not a side effect.

It is part of the Fruit's identity.

Natural Backlash

A Backlash is instead a consequence that using the power causes in its owner's body.

Examples:

  • dehydration;
  • cooling of the body;
  • pain caused by extreme transformations;
  • temporary loss of sensation;
  • overheating.

The Backlash must be defined in the Sheet if it is truly a characteristic of the Fruit.

The GM must not invent it during a scene solely to restrict an unexpected use.

Guide to Devil Fruit Talents

Fruit Talents represent specific developments in mastery of the power.

The Identity Sheet establishes what the Fruit can naturally do. Talents expand those possibilities, specialise them, turn them into mechanical advantages or overcome some initial limitations.

Each Talent includes:

Manual Text
The description currently found in the rules.

Chapter 4 ·

Paramecia

Paramecia

The unique power

Paramecia encompass powers that differ enormously. They can alter the body, produce matter or affect the environment.

Their fundamental principle is creative freedom: the player and Game Master define a recognisable power and develop its applications without turning it into an unlimited collection of unrelated abilities.

4.1 · Trait · The Unique Power

Always active

The character alters the body, matter or the environment through a power agreed with the Game Master.

The Fruit's nature determines what the character can do.

There is no universal table assigning every Paramecia the same set of effects.

The Trait represents the Fruit's fundamental properties, not an unlock to purchase.

4.2 · Signature · The Power’s Mark

Clean Hit · Margin +4

When the character scores a Clean Hit with a Fruit Technique, the power leaves an additional consequence consistent with its nature.

This might be a change to the terrain, a status or a zone remaining after impact.

The specific application is defined with the Game Master according to the power possessed.

The Signature does not grant the player permanent access to every effect in the Catalogue: it represents the Clean Hit's special consequence.

If the Signature's consequence is a Status that allows a Save, the Threshold is determined by the Fruit Die (§1.4 of the Game Manual), because the Status is produced by the Signature rather than selected as a Technique effect. A Clean Hit with the Technique is still required.

4.3 · d4–d6 · The Gift

Versatile Power

Manual Text

Versatile Power
Passive
You unlock a new application of the Fruit.
You might learn to use the power at a distance, apply it to your body or use it to defend yourself.
The new application lets you develop an entire family of Techniques you could not previously build.
The choice must be consistent with the Fruit's original concept.

Purpose

Versatile Power expands the Paramecia's initial possibilities.

It does not directly increase the Fruit's damage, reach or power. Instead, it lets you develop a new way of using an existing power.

Definitive clarification

When the Talent is acquired, the player chooses a new Fruit Application with the GM and adds it to the Identity Sheet.

The Application must logically derive from the Power’s Core.

Example:

A Fruit initially allows wax to be produced from the user's body.

Versatile Power could allow the user to:

  • remotely control wax already produced;
  • use wax as protection;
  • coat their own body in wax.

The Talent grants one of these new directions, not all of them at once.

It does not allow new properties to be freely invented every time it is used.

Fine Control

Manual Text

Fine Control
Passive · Outside combat
Add +1 die to appropriate manipulation, traversal, protection and stealth checks when using the Fruit.
The bonus applies only to activities in which the power actually provides an advantage.

Purpose

Represents the precision achieved in everyday use of the Fruit.

It is mainly intended for exploration, infiltration, manipulating the environment and creative uses that are not directly offensive.

Definitive clarification

Fine Control does not grant new Applications.

The character must already be able to perform the action according to their Fruit Sheet.

When a check is required and the power actually provides an advantage, the character adds:

+1 die

to the appropriate check.

Example:

A character capable of manipulating threads could use Fine Control to operate a small mechanism with extreme precision.

However, if their Fruit cannot directly control people's bodies, Fine Control does not allow them to do so.

Passive Gift

Manual Text

Passive Gift
Passive · Permanent
The Fruit grants you a permanent property consistent with its nature.
This property does not require Stamina or the use of a Technique.
For example, a rubber body does not conduct electricity.
The player and GM agree exactly which property is gained and what consequences it produces.
Once defined, it remains a permanent characteristic of the character.

Purpose

Allows a natural consequence of the power to become a permanent property of the character.

Definitive clarification

The property is chosen when the Talent is acquired and recorded in the Fruit Sheet.

It must derive directly from the power's nature.

For example:

Rubber body → does not normally conduct electricity.

Body made from a buoyant substance → may naturally have particular buoyancy, subject to the normal weaknesses of Fruits.

The property cannot freely equate to:

  • general immunity to damage;
  • permanent Attribute bonuses;
  • existing Talents;
  • abilities that do not truly derive from the Fruit's nature.

Once chosen, it does not change from one scene to another.

Power Reflex

Manual Text

Power Reflex
Reaction
1 time per combat
0 ST
When you or an ally within 5 metres are about to be attacked, the Fruit reacts instinctively.
The damage is reduced by one die.
No Technique is required: the power manifests directly through the Reaction.

Purpose

Represents an instinctive defensive manifestation of the Paramecia.

The power intervenes immediately to lessen an attack directed at the user or a nearby companion.

Definitive clarification

The phrase:

“The damage is reduced by one die”

means:

all the attack's Damage Dice decrease by 1 Grade.

Progression:

d20 → d12
d12 → d10
d10 → d8
d8 → d6
d6 → d4

d4 is the minimum and does not decrease any further.

Example:

An attack deals:

3d10

Power Reflex turns it into:

3d8

An attack that deals:

d12 + 2d8

becomes:

d10 + 2d6

The Talent modifies the attack's Damage Dice, not the attack roll.

It does not cancel any Statuses, Displacement or other attack effects unless they expressly depend on the damage.

It uses the character's Reaction and can be activated only once per combat.

4.4 · d8–d10 · Control

Dual Use

Manual Text

Dual Use
2 ST
Active Talent · Bonus Action
You can apply the power twice during the same Action.
You can choose two targets, two different points on the field, or apply it twice to the same target.
The use must remain compatible with the power you possess and the Technique performed.

Purpose

Allows the Paramecia to use both the combat and creative aspects of its power at the same time.

Definitive clarification

The text is clarified as follows:

during the same Action you can make two applications of the Fruit.

Only one of the two can be:

  • an attack;
  • an offensive Technique;
  • an application that deals damage;
  • an application that directly produces an offensive Status.

The second application must be narrative or utility-based.

It can, for example:

  • move an object;
  • generate a small quantity of its substance;
  • modify an element of the scenery;
  • manipulate something already controlled;
  • use a non-offensive property of the Fruit.

Dual Use does not grant:

  • two attacks;
  • two Techniques;
  • two offensive applications on the same target;
  • two instances of damage.

The Talent costs:

2 ST

once only.

Any offensive Technique pays all its costs normally.

The second application pays a separate cost only if the ability used already has one.

What Remains

Manual Text

What Remains
Passive
The durations of Fruit Techniques improve without additional costs.

One turn → becomes three turns, without paying the additional 2 ST.

Maintain → the first turn of upkeep is free.

The Talent modifies only the durations to which it applies.

Purpose

Specialises the Paramecia in effects that remain on the field or continue acting after their initial use.

Definitive clarification

What Remains modifies only a mechanical duration provided by the Technique.

It does not automatically make anything created by the Fruit permanent.

If a Technique lasts:

1 turn

it instead lasts:

3 turns.

If a Technique requires:

Maintain

the first upkeep cost is not paid.

If the Technique has no duration or upkeep, the Talent provides no benefit.

The matter's natural permanence specified in the Fruit Sheet remains a separate property.

None of My Own

Manual Text

None of My Own
Passive
The Eye of the Storm effect becomes more accessible to your Fruit Techniques.
When you use it:
· It occupies no slots.
· It costs no Stamina.
· It can be selected even before the normal d12 requirement.
The Fruit recognises the character's companions and allows them to be excluded from the effects of their Area Techniques.
Eye of the Storm does not itself grant access to the Area Form: it applies only to a Fruit Technique that already has a valid Area according to the power's nature and the Source's rules.

Purpose

Allows the development of a Paramecia particularly precise in using Area Techniques.

Definitive clarification

When using their own Fruit Technique with an Area already authorised by the Source, the character can select Eye of the Storm:

  • without occupying Slots;
  • without spending ST;
  • regardless of the normal d12 requirement.

The player chooses which targets within the Area are excluded from the Technique.

The Talent protects against the Technique's direct consequences.

It does not automatically make allies immune to independent environmental consequences that might later arise from the scene.

For example, being excluded from a fire explosion does not automatically grant immunity to an ordinary building that catches fire after the attack.

4.5 · d12 · Mastery

Natural Reach

Manual Text

Natural Reach
Passive
Your Fruit has reached a level of control that makes projecting the power more efficient.
You gain three benefits:
· Range effects in Fruit Techniques are free and occupy no slots.
· Area Techniques increase their radius by one step.
· The Chain effect costs 1 ST less.
The Talent does not automatically grant Range to a Technique without it. It removes the cost of the Range actually selected.
Natural Reach does not create a Range or Area the Fruit could not normally produce. The benefits apply only to Fruit Techniques that already have Range, Area or Chain validly authorised by the Source.

Purpose

Makes a Paramecia specialising in projecting its power at a distance or onto multiple targets much more efficient.

Definitive clarification

Natural Reach modifies Techniques, not the Ordinary reach on the Fruit Sheet.

A Technique must still have Range, an Area or Chain validly authorised by the Source to benefit from the corresponding improvement.

If it does:

  • Range costs 0 ST;
  • it occupies no Slots.

Area Techniques increase by one step of the normal template progression, not by an arbitrary amount chosen by the GM.

Chapter 5 ·

Guidelines · Combat Form

Guidelines · Combat Form

The Combat Form represents a particular state in which the character continuously uses their Fruit on themselves to modify their way of fighting.

It is not a Technique.

It is not a new Fruit Application.

It is not a reduced form of Awakening.

The Combat Form takes a possibility already present in the Fruit Identity Sheet and focuses it on the character's body, movements or way of fighting.

The Talent must therefore produce a primarily personal benefit.

5.1 · Creating a Combat Form

When the Talent is acquired, the player and Game Master define the Form together.

The following must be established:

Name

The Form's name.

Manifestation

How the character changes while using the Fruit this way.

Benefit

The concrete advantage gained while the Form is active.

Cost

How much Stamina activation or upkeep requires.

Limitations

Any conditions required to retain its benefits.

Once defined, the Form stays the same.

The character does not choose a different benefit each time they activate it.

5.2 · The fundamental principle

The Combat Form primarily concerns:

the character themselves.

It can modify:

  • their own body;
  • their mobility;
  • their offensive ability;
  • their defensive ability;
  • the way they personally use the Fruit;
  • or another characteristic directly linked to their combat style.

It is not intended to modify the Fruit's general functioning on a large scale.

5.3 · What a Combat Form is not

A Combat Form does not normally allow the user to:

  • extend the power across the entire environment;
  • gain new categories of targets;
  • transform large quantities of surrounding matter;
  • freely change the origin point of Techniques;
  • control vast areas;
  • permanently alter the battlefield;
  • overcome important Structural limitations of the Fruit;
  • gain a manifestation equivalent to Awakening.

These developments belong to different abilities and, at the highest levels, to Awakening.

5.4 · Defining the benefit

There is no universal table of Combat Form benefits.

Two completely different Paramecia should not be forced to choose the same bonuses.

The benefit is agreed between the player and Game Master and must derive directly from the chosen Manifestation.

The question to use during creation is:

“How does constantly using this power on my character concretely modify their way of fighting?”

The answer determines the benefit.

Assessing the benefit

The benefit must be significant, because Combat Form is a d12 Talent, but must remain less important than an Awakening.

The GM should consider:

  • how often it comes into play;
  • how much it modifies attack, defence or movement;
  • how many of the character's abilities it influences simultaneously;
  • how easily it can be maintained;
  • how significant the required Stamina cost is.

A very specific benefit can have a modest cost.

A benefit influencing much of combat should require a greater commitment.

5.5 · Activation

The Combat Form is activated using:

Bonus Action

Its duration and any upkeep are established when it is created.

It can normally remain active for the entire scene as long as the character can sustain its cost.

If the character can no longer pay the specified upkeep, the Form ends.

5.6 · Combat Form and Techniques

The Form modifies the character.

Techniques still determine the specific actions they perform.

Being in Combat Form does not mean being able to apply the following for free:

  • damage;
  • Statuses;
  • Zones;
  • Displacement;
  • additional attacks;
  • or other Builder effects.

If the character wants to produce one of these effects, they still use an appropriate Technique.

The Form can improve how that Technique is performed only if the agreed benefit provides for it.

5.7 · Combat Form and the Fruit Sheet

The Form must respect the Power’s Core and Applications already possessed.

It cannot be used as a shortcut to obtain Versatile Power or Second Nature.

If the Fruit cannot normally do something, the Form does not automatically grant that ability.

It can instead raise an already possible personal use to a higher level.

5.8 · Example · Spring Body

The character possesses a Paramecia that allows parts of their body to be transformed into springs.

They develop:

Compressed Spring

Manifestation

The character continuously keeps their body under tension, compressing limbs and muscles through the Fruit.

Benefit

The player and GM agree on an advantage linked to more explosive movements and attacks.

Cost

A cost consistent with the benefit's effectiveness is defined.

The Form does not allow the character to transform the environment into springs.

It does not modify other characters.

It does not expand the Fruit's general radius.

It enhances only the way the user fights through their own body.

5.9 · Example · Wax Armour

The character can produce and shape wax.

They develop a Form in which their body is constantly covered in a reinforced layer.

Manifestation

The wax forms armour shaped to the body.

Benefit

An appropriate defensive advantage is agreed.

Cost

Maintaining the armour requires an agreed amount of Stamina.

The Talent does not automatically allow the entire crew to be protected or the whole battlefield to be covered.

That would be an application on a different scale.

5.10 · Combat Form and Awakening

The fundamental difference is:

Combat Form

The character brings their Fruit into their combat style.

The effect is centred on themselves.

Awakening

The Fruit exceeds some of its normal boundaries and changes the power's relationship with the environment, targets or normal Limitations.

The effect becomes centred on the power itself.

Guidelines · Paramecia Awakening

Guidelines · Paramecia Awakening

Awakening represents the maximum evolution of the Fruit's Core.

It is not simply a numerically superior version of normal Techniques.

The power's fundamental principle achieves a scale, freedom or depth that is normally impossible.

Awakening is designed with the Game Master when the character meets the necessary requirements.

It must be fully defined before its first activation.

It is not invented during the battle.

Once approved, it becomes that Fruit's specific Awakening.

6.1 · Building the Awakening

Every Awakening must define:

Name

Identifies the awakened manifestation.

Awakened Principle

States which fundamental characteristic of the Fruit is taken beyond its normal limits.

Manifestation

Describes what happens when Awakening is activated.

Development Families

State which aspects of the Fruit are transformed.

Benefits

Define the agreed mechanical effects.

Area or Reach

Establishes the space within which Awakening can operate, when relevant.

Valid targets

Establishes what can be influenced.

Permanence

Defines what happens to the changes produced when Awakening ends.

Limitations

States what remains impossible.

6.2 · Awakening Families

Families do not grant bonuses.

They identify which boundary of the Fruit is overcome by Awakening.

Origin

Modifies the way or point from which the power can manifest.

Costs / Discounts

Modifies the efficiency with which certain Fruit abilities are used.

Expansion

Increases the scale on which the power can operate.

This can concern reach, quantity, area, number of targets or other similar characteristics.

Domain

Expands the relationship between the Fruit and compatible matter, environments or phenomena.

Defence

Takes the power's defensive properties beyond normal mastery.

Attack

Brings the Fruit's offensive application to a higher manifestation.

Control

Increases the precision, duration, complexity or quantity of controllable manifestations.

6.3 · Families are not benefits

If an Awakening belongs to the family:

Domain

this does not mean it automatically gains a particular bonus.

It means the Awakening primarily overcomes a limitation relating to control of the environment.

The specific benefit must be written separately.

The same Family can therefore produce completely different Awakenings.

6.4 · Multiple Families

An Awakening can involve multiple Families simultaneously when its Manifestation requires it.

The number of Families does not automatically establish its power.

A single major breakthrough can be more influential than several minor improvements.

The GM must assess the Awakening as a whole.

6.5 · Manifestation and benefit

The Manifestation describes:

what becomes possible.

The Benefit establishes:

what changes mechanically.

The two must be defined separately.

For example:

Manifestation

The character can transfer their Fruit's property to the environment.

This does not automatically establish damage, Statuses, bonuses or penalties.

These must be defined through agreed Benefits or the Techniques used.

6.6 · Awakening does not replace Techniques

Awakening enormously expands the Fruit's possibilities.

It does not turn every narrative use into a free combat effect.

If the character wants to obtain:

  • damage;
  • Statuses;
  • immobilisation;
  • Zones;
  • Displacement;
  • or other effects governed by the system;
  • they must use an appropriate Technique or possess an Awakening Benefit explicitly establishing that consequence.

6.7 · Living creatures

Awakening does not automatically grant the ability to directly modify living beings.

This ability must derive from the Fruit or be explicitly provided by Awakening.

When used against a hostile target, Defence, Saves, opposed checks or other appropriate rules normally continue to apply.

Awakening does not allow the target's defences to be automatically ignored.

6.8 · Permanence

Every Awakening must establish what happens to its manifestations when the effect ends.

The changes can:

  • disappear;
  • gradually return to normal;
  • remain physically present;
  • or produce permanent environmental consequences.

The choice depends on the Fruit and must be defined during design.

Narrative permanence does not automatically extend Statuses, Zones or other mechanical effects.

6.9 · Limitations

Awakening can overcome some of the Fruit's Structural limitations.

It must specify which ones, however.

All Limitations not expressly modified continue to apply.

The fundamental weaknesses of Fruits always remain in effect.

6.10 · The Price of Awakening

Awakening normally lasts:

1 scene.

At the end:

  • the character's Stamina drops to 0;
  • the character cannot use the Fruit until their next Long Rest.

Voluntarily ending Awakening before the scene ends does not avoid the specified price.

Zoan

Zoan

Zoan allow the owner to transform into an animal creature and combine its characteristics with their own body.

Their development is primarily linked to:

  • physical strength;
  • resilience;
  • mobility;
  • senses;
  • natural weapons;
  • instincts;
  • the creature's biological characteristics.

Every Zoan has three fundamental forms:

Human Form
The character's ordinary appearance.

Hybrid Form
Combines human anatomy with the creature's characteristics.

Beast Form
Fully assumes the creature's form.

When defining a Zoan, the player and Game Master complete a brief Creature Sheet.

8.2 · Anatomy and mechanical advantages

The creature's physical characteristics actually exist when the character assumes an appropriate form.

A bird Zoan has wings.

A feline Zoan has claws.

A horned Zoan truly has those horns.

A Zoan with a shell truly has that structure.

This grants the normal narrative possibilities arising from the anatomy.

It does not automatically grant numerical bonuses.

For example:

  • having wings allows flight, but does not automatically increase Movement;
  • having claws allows their physical use, but does not automatically make them Natural Weapons of a Grade equal to the Fruit;
  • having a shell does not automatically grant Damage Reduction;
  • having a developed sense of smell does not automatically allow hidden creatures to be located within a fixed distance.

Talents turn these natural characteristics into specific mechanical advantages.

8.3 · Trait · The Three Forms

Free

  • occupies no Effect Slots;

In Hybrid or Beast Form, the body assumes the creature's appropriate anatomical characteristics.

Dominant Characteristic

Every Zoan Awakening also takes one fundamental characteristic of the creature to its extreme.

This is defined using the normal Awakening Guidelines.

When appropriate, it can concern:

Movement

A creature built for speed or flight can attain exceptional movement abilities.

Defence

An armoured, resilient or particularly hard-to-defeat creature can develop superior defence.

Size

A naturally enormous creature can take its mass, reach and physical presence to an even greater extreme.

Senses

A creature specialising in perception can develop exceptional sensory abilities.

Body control

An extremely agile, flexible or adaptable creature can gain superior mastery of its own body.

Heritage

An Ancient or Mythical Zoan that has already unlocked its Heritage can take that ability to a higher manifestation.

The specific benefit is defined when Awakening is designed and remains fixed.

Zoan Awakening Identity

Zoan Awakening does not grant a universal increase to Strength or Movement.

Each creature instead develops what makes it truly exceptional.

A feline can achieve extreme speed.

An armoured creature can develop extraordinary defence.

A large herbivore can enormously increase its mass and trampling ability.

A bird can take its aerial mobility beyond normal limits.

A Mythical Zoan can take its supernatural Heritage to a new manifestation.

This way, two different Zoan produce truly different Awakenings.
