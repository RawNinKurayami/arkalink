Chapter 01

Entering Prestige

Prestige marks a character’s entry into the ranks of the greatest in their field. The experience accumulated during the voyage takes shape in abilities that make legendary feats possible. A blow can reshape the ground of a battlefield; in the hands of an exceptional swordsman, even an ordinary blade becomes a fearsome weapon. Aboard ship, the expertise of whoever holds the helm or tends the wounded can decide the fate of the entire crew.

This growth preserves the character’s identity. Someone who built their style on physical power continues to express it through strength. Someone who relies on movement, precision or specialist knowledge takes those qualities to ever greater heights. Prestige makes the path they have followed recognisable and lets them explore its possibilities to their peak.

Every Role can achieve this excellence through its own craft. A carpenter expresses it in the ship they build and keep afloat. An archaeologist demonstrates it by reading what others cannot interpret. For a captain, it can mean leading the crew at a moment when a well-given order changes the outcome of the battle. Prestige feats also belong to those who make the voyage possible.

1.1Growth beyond d20

In die-based progressions, Prestige continues growth beyond d20. The die retains the d20 and gains an additional die, which increases through six stages: d20+d4, d20+d6, d20+d8, d20+d10, d20+d12 and d20+d20. The first five are called Prestige I, II, III, IV and V; the sixth is named Saikyō. The larger scales are reached gradually as you progress towards this peak.

This progression improves the relevant rolls and powers the effects that depend on the die reached. Where a rule uses the full roll, the additional die is also included. Where it specifies a distance, radius or another value linked to Prestige, use the scale of the ability concerned.

Passive Defence and Prestige

If the Racial Attribute enters Prestige, Passive Defence also continues to grow. PD uses the full Grade of the Racial Attribute: add together the maximum values of its component dice and add +1. No roll is made.

When a graded source that has reached Prestige imposes a Save, it does not roll its own pool. The Prestige Grade determines a fixed Threshold according to the following table. This also applies to Statuses applied by evolutions, such as Unstoppable Maul and Seismic Wave. An effect included in a Technique instead uses the Technique’s Grade, unless a specific rule says otherwise.

Each Attribute, Skill or Haki Colour retains its own progression. A character can excel in one area while still being a beginner in another. This combination defines their speciality.

1.2Attributes and Role Skills

Attribute Prestige develops the qualities on which the character builds their feats. Strength can expand destruction and launches; Technique can support superhuman movement and agility; Cunning can take control of shots to exceptional levels. Additional benefits follow Role compatibility and the conditions of the ability used.

This is why two characters with the same Attribute can express Prestige in different ways. A Striker’s Strength manifests in impacts, while a Swordsman develops their ability to cleave through the Talents provided by their path. Technique mobility can belong to Striker and Swordsman; other Roles use the benefits compatible with their specialisation. The Attribute die nevertheless remains available for the normal rolls provided by the rules.

Role Skill Prestige instead grants access to the Talents that make the abilities of a profession extraordinary. You acquire one Talent at d20+d4, a second at d20+d8 and a third at d20+d12. The order is free, subject to prerequisites. At Saikyō, your acquired Talents improve.

The additional upgrades from Strength, Technique and Cunning do not consume these choices. Attribute and Skill can grow at different Grades: a high Attribute expands the possibilities that belong to it, while a high Skill opens choices from its catalogue. Sharing a Skill with another specialisation does not automatically grant its Talents.

Synergies in Prestige

Synergies between Attributes and Skills continue to be checked beyond d20. A Synergy can use any Skill allowed by its requirement, even if it is not a Role Skill. Intermediate Prestige Grades do not grant new Unique Traits and do not consume Role Skill Prestige Talent choices.

The full Synergy rules and the ordinary Unique Trait catalogue are provided in §16.9 of the Game Manual.

1.3Spirit and Haki

Spirit Prestige develops the character’s determination and offers three upgrades shared by all Roles. Choices occur at d20+d4, d20+d8 and d20+d12. These abilities let you express your will through mental resistance, coordinated Colour activation and PIP recovery according to their respective rules.

Armament and Observation cannot exceed the Spirit die, including its Prestige. Spirit raises the attainable limit, while each Colour grows along its own path. Their Prestige upgrades existing active effects; ordinary passives retain their function. The pools retain their established maximums: 5 Armament PIP, 5 Observation PIP and 3 Conqueror’s PIP.

The Sniper’s Observation Role Skill and Observation Haki remain two separate progressions.

Conqueror’s Haki follows a separate progression consisting of Prestige I, Prestige II and Saikyō. It has no die and requires no numerical Spirit thresholds. The GM assigns these stages in relation to the character’s development in the campaign.

1.4Prestige feats at the table

Prestige expands what a character can attempt and the effectiveness of acquired abilities. To use them, declare the intention and form of the intervention, then apply the relevant rules. Activations, reaches and use limits continue to guide choices during battle. Materials and preparation retain their importance in professional activities.

A large scale describes the possible scope of a feat. Actual destruction depends on the impact and the resilience of the environment; creatures are affected according to the attack or Talent used. Similarly, a legendary ship comes from work in a shipyard, and an extraordinary dish must actually be prepared. The ability to achieve exceptional results is expressed through specific actions.

Resources make these actions meaningful. Role Talents, Spirit upgrades and Haki effects share the normal availability of Bonus Actions. The player decides when to use their most powerful Talent, which abilities to accompany it with, and how many resources to keep for the next moment.

1.5Saikyō

Saikyō is the peak of a specific Prestige path. In die-based progressions it corresponds to d20+d20; for Conqueror’s Haki it is the third stage assigned by the GM. A character can reach it in their Role Skill while other areas continue to grow.

At this level, already acquired Talents and upgrades reach their final form. Saikyō does not grant a fourth Talent. Ultimates retain their own rules: the system introduces no dedicated Prestige evolutions.

Prestige Unique Traits

Synergies have no Trait progression at individual Prestige Grades. When both the Attribute and Skill of the same Synergy reach Saikyō, that pair can make a single Prestige Unique Trait available. This Trait is not a fourth Role Talent and follows its own catalogue. Until that catalogue is published, reaching Saikyō does not automatically grant a new effect.

The character continues to carry the choices that shaped them. Their abilities can now affect a battle, a crossing or the crew’s survival with a force that once seemed unreachable. Prestige allows those feats to be played and to become part of the story of their own voyage.

Chapter 02

Attribute Prestige

Prestige develops the character through two separate progressions. Attribute Prestige upgrades the abilities of compatible Roles and determines scales for destruction, launching, mobility, cutting, range and trajectory control. Role Skill Prestige grants the choices used to acquire the Prestige Talents of your Role.

Attribute upgrades are gained on reaching the required threshold and do not consume Talent choices. Using them still requires a compatible ability, with its effects, slots, actions and costs. Skill Talents, however, are acquired individually: reaching a threshold does not grant the entire catalogue.

The two progressions can be at different Grades. A high Attribute expands physical possibilities without granting Skill Talents; a high Skill grants choices without automatically raising Attribute scales. In this text, the Technique Attribute means the character’s characteristic; a Technique means a move built with its own effects and costs. ST means Stamina and SP means Structure Points.

2.2Part one: Attribute Prestige

2.3Strength Prestige

For Striker and Crusher, Strength in Prestige makes the environmental destruction scale and Colossal Launch available. They are passive Attribute upgrades, with an additional cost of 0 ST and no Talent choice. Destruction concerns a Strength-based impact or an ability that expressly uses its scale, such as the Striker’s Titanic Wave Talent and the Crusher’s Seismic Wave. Colossal Launch requires an offensive melee Technique of your own Role containing the Builder’s Launch effect. All costs of the Technique and effects used remain payable.

This only serves to distinguish it from the Striker’s Launch Talent and other abilities that produce their own push.

Environmental destruction

The destruction scale expresses the maximum extent of the environmental consequences of a Strength blow: broken paving, gouged ground, torn-off doors, shattered cover and damaged parts of structures. The impact point is the centre of the area.

The radius indicates how far the devastation can extend. Actual destruction depends on the blow, material, resilience of the obstacle and damage dealt. A strong wall can remain standing even if it lies within the maximum area. The scale does not automatically destroy every object in the area.

For structures with Structure Points, abbreviated SP, subtract the damage actually suffered and apply the specified consequences when they give way. For elements without SP, the GM determines breakage and collapse consistent with the damage and material. When an impact propagates through the area, damage decreases with distance according to the following bands.

For Striker and Crusher the scale limits the consequences for the environment; the blow or effect used determines which targets receive numerical damage. Dealing the blow’s damage to creatures in an area requires the Striker’s Titanic Wave Talent, the Crusher’s Seismic Wave or a Technique that provides for it. Prestige Strength alone does not assign damage to all creatures within the radius.

A Crash can also produce cracks, debris and breakage compatible with this scale. Its damage remains as established by Colossal Launch and is applied only to the recipients specified in the first collision rule.

Activation conditions for environmental destruction

Type: passive Strength Attribute upgrade. Roles: Striker and Crusher. Requirement: Strength of at least d20+d4 and a compatible impact on the environment. Additional cost: 0 ST. Additional action: none.

You may use the scale when you land a Strength-based attack and the impact is actually delivered to the ground, an object or a structure. It can be a basic attack or an offensive Technique of your Role: a Striker can punch the ground; a Crusher can bring their hammer down on the floor or against a wall. For the Crusher, the attack must use a compatible bludgeoning weapon.

A blow against a creature can also affect the environment if the impact is transmitted to a surface or structure. Identify the contact that produces the devastation: hitting an opponent backed against a wall, delivering the blow into the ground beneath them, or making them collide with a surface through the specified effects. Simply hitting a creature does not automatically damage the entire surrounding environment.

The scale expresses the ability to extend an actual impact. A missed or avoided blow does not automatically produce an area of damage. If a collision with the environment nevertheless occurs during resolution, that collision must be recognised and resolved with its own damage; it is not assumed to have occurred merely because Strength is in Prestige.

Environmental destruction alone does not require acquiring a Skill Prestige Talent, and does not require Launch or Crash in the Technique. These effects become necessary when you want to hurl a creature and deal the collision damage described in the following sections. A normal Strength check to lift, drag or push an object does not automatically deal the damage of an attack.

Environmental procedure

Before rolling, declare the point you want to hit and, if you want to propagate devastation, the chosen extent within your Strength’s maximum radius. Resolve the attack with its normal reach, required actions and any Technique costs. An undefended surface requires no additional check to activate the upgrade; any rolls needed to reach or hit it follow the ordinary rules.

When the impact occurs, use the blow’s damage as D and apply to the structures reached the band for their distance from the impact point. For a Crash, instead use only its Strength roll, respecting the limit on recipients of the first collision. Do not make an additional Strength roll to determine the radius or add environmental damage that is not provided for.

For structures with SP, subtract the actual damage after the relevant reductions. The Crusher’s Demolisher and the Striker’s Breaking Fists, if acquired, retain their benefits against compatible objects; attenuation bands continue to apply. For elements without SP the GM determines consequences consistent with the damage and material.

The impact must be able to reach the elements involved: an obstacle that withstands it can stop its propagation. The maximum radius does not guarantee that every structure in the area breaks. You may choose a smaller extent, and a particular ability can define the zone differently, such as the cone of Seismic Wave.

Damage to creatures and Talent activation

Environmental destruction alone applies propagated damage to the environment. Creatures receive the attack’s direct damage only if they are targets of the ability used. Collapses, falls and other hazards produced by the breaking environment can still injure them under the normal environmental rules; they are not an automatic copy of the punch or hammer damage.

A Striker with the Titanic Wave Talent may, after a successful blow of their own on their turn, spend the Bonus Action and 2 ST to also strike the other creatures in the chosen radial area. A Crusher with Seismic Wave may, after a Strength bludgeoning blow with valid contact on the environment on their turn, spend the Bonus Action and 3 ST to attack the creatures resting on the surface in the chosen cone. Both use the same Strength scale, with the limits and defences described in their respective Talents.

A Talent or Technique granting an area attack also determines which creatures you hit. The Strength scale does not automatically enlarge the area of any Technique: a move must expressly reference this scale to use it against creatures. Its actions, effects, slots and costs remain necessary.

Structures reached by the same impact receive the damage for their band only once. The passive scale and the Talent propagating that blow do not generate two copies of damage on the same object or creature.

Striker example: with Strength d20+d10 you strike the ground and deal 40 damage. The maximum radius is 100 m. Paving at 40 m may receive 20 environmental damage, according to its resilience and SP; a guard at that distance does not automatically receive that 20 damage. If, after the blow, you activate Titanic Wave by paying the Bonus Action and 2 ST, the guard reached by the wave may defend: if hit, they also receive the 20 damage specified by their band. The paving does not receive another 20 damage from the second description of the same impact.

Area Form and impact propagation

A Technique with the Area Form and the propagation of an impact are different mechanics. Swordsman and Crusher have only Close Area (d8, 2 ST), centred on the character with a radius equal to the weapon’s effective Reach. Striker and Sniper have no ordinary Style Area Form.

Different areas require a special Source that expressly grants them. An Area Technique retains its own shape, effects, slots and costs.

The environmental destruction of Strength Prestige originates from a single identifiable physical impact on the ground, an object or a structure. An Area does not generate an impact point for every creature and does not multiply the destruction scale; resolve a single valid point. The scale retains its own radius and rules.

Damage by distance

This rule applies to damage propagated by the Striker’s Titanic Wave Talent, the Crusher’s Seismic Wave and environmental destruction in the area. Let D be the total damage of the blow generating the impact, before the initial target’s reductions. It includes the Striker’s Titan’s Power, the Crusher’s additional Strength roll from Unstoppable Maul and other bonuses applicable to the blow; it excludes any subsequent Crash. Let R be the maximum radius established by Strength Prestige.

Measure the distance of each creature or structure from the impact point and apply the corresponding band.

Distance from the impact pointPropagated damage
Up to one quarter of R, inclusiveD
Beyond one quarter and up to half of R, inclusiveHalf of D
Beyond half and up to three quarters of R, inclusiveOne quarter of D

Colossal Launch

Type: passive Strength Prestige upgrade. Roles: Striker and Crusher. Additional cost: 0 ST. Use requirement: an offensive melee Technique of your Role with the Launch effect. For collision damage: the same Technique must also contain Crash.

When a compatible Technique hits and applies Launch, you may replace the 3 m specified by the ordinary effect with the maximum distance for your Strength Prestige. Declare the launch direction and chosen distance, up to the maximum allowed by the scale.

With Strength d20+d8 you can hurl the target up to 20 m; with Strength d20+d12, up to 50 m. You may choose a shorter launch. The target’s mass and size must be compatible with what your Strength allows you to hurl: the scale in metres does not by itself grant the ability to lift any weight.

Launch distance is measured from the position of the target hit. It is the movement caused after the blow, not the attack’s reach: you must first reach and hit the target with the Technique’s normal reach.

This use modifies the Launch effect contained in the Technique and does not require the target to already be Restrained. The ordinary Talent called Launch, which throws an already restrained enemy up to 5 m, retains its conditions when used separately. Having it does not replace the effect that must be present in the Technique.

Colossal Launch does not grant a launch on any basic attack and does not add the required effects to the Technique for free. Launch occupies its own Effect slot and retains its ordinary cost of 1 ST. Crash, if present, occupies another slot and retains its ordinary cost of 2 ST. The Prestige upgrade has an additional cost of 0 ST, but does not make the Technique free, increase slots or remove other requirements.

Obstacles can interrupt the launch before the declared distance. If the first obstacle gives way, the trajectory can continue within the remaining distance; if it withstands the impact, the target stops against it. Any further collisions do not repeat this ability’s additional damage.

Crash with Colossal Launch

When the Technique contains both Launch and Crash, the hurled target’s first collision with a creature or surface triggers Crash damage. Landing on the ground can constitute this first collision.

Crash damage = one full Strength roll.

Roll the Strength die once, including the Prestige die, and add the results. With Strength d20+d8, Crash damage is therefore the result of d20+d8. This roll replaces the normal +1d8 from Crash: the two are not added together.

Apply the same result once to each of the following recipients:

  • The hurled target, as additional damage on top of the Technique’s initial damage.
  • The first creature or structure actually struck, as impact damage.

The hurled target has already suffered the attack that launches them; at the first collision, apply the relevant reductions to Crash damage. A second creature involved can defend normally against the original Technique’s attack roll result: if they avoid the collision, they receive no damage and do not count as the first actual collision. If hit, they receive only the Strength roll, with their own normal reductions.

An obstacle with SP loses Structure Points equal to the Crash damage actually received. The Technique’s initial damage is not copied onto the obstacle or a second creature.

The Crash roll does not receive Titan’s Power, the Technique’s Damage Die or Haki damage bonuses again. Its formula remains a single full Strength roll. The launch length and number of objects along the trajectory do not add multipliers.

You may apply this Crash damage only once per use of the Technique, at the first actual collision. Passing through or meeting other obstacles does not generate new copies of the roll. Any other distinct environmental hazards remain governed by their own rules.

Launch without Crash

A Technique with Launch but without Crash deals its own normal damage and can hurl the target to the distance allowed by Strength. Colossal Launch does not grant it additional collision damage, either against the target or against what they encounter.

Complete collision example

A Striker has Strength d20+d8 and uses a Technique with Launch and Crash. Their maximum launch is 20 m. The Technique hits and deals 30 initial damage, already including any bonuses to the blow.

The Striker chooses to hurl the enemy towards a wall 12 m from the target’s starting position. The enemy strikes the wall. For Crash, the Striker rolls d20+d8 and gets a total of 18.

  • The enemy receives 30 damage from the Technique and 18 from Crash, for a total of 48 before any reductions.
  • The wall receives 18 structural damage. If it has 40 SP and no relevant reduction, it drops to 22 SP.

The wall withstands the impact, so the launch ends at 12 m. It does not receive the 30 initial damage, and the normal +1d8 from Crash is not added. If it instead gave way and the trajectory continued, Crash damage would still already have been applied once.

The same resolution applies to a Crusher using a compatible Technique with Launch and Crash. The upgraded launch depends on Strength, regardless of the Skill that unlocked any Role Talents.

Impossible footholds

Example: you are already in the air and have Technique Attribute d20+d12. You push off with an aerial Leap and rise up to 50 m, using part of the 100 m of special displacement available. 3.3Striker · Shared Prestige Talents

Athletics choices upgrade the damage of unarmed blows and allow their impact to propagate. Endless Flurry is described in the section shared by both paths.

Titan’s Power

Type: passive. Additional cost: 0 ST. Application: unarmed basic attacks and Striker offensive Techniques performed unarmed.

When a compatible attack of yours hits, roll your entire Strength die once and add the result to the blow’s normal damage. If Strength has a Prestige die, roll both dice and add the results.

With Strength d20+d12, for example, the additional damage is d20+d12. This benefit applies to every successful blow, including additional attacks granted by Endless Flurry and compatible Reaction attacks.

The additional damage roll is separate from the attack roll. Titan’s Power does not add the attack check result a second time and does not by itself change the blow’s reach, number of targets or area. Normal damage reductions apply.

When another ability uses this blow’s total damage, it also includes the contribution from Titan’s Power. When an ability requires only a Strength roll, such as the Crash from Colossal Launch, do not add this Talent again.

Example: a Technique deals 12 damage before this Talent. With Strength d20+d8 you get 14 on the d20 and 5 on the d8: add 19, and total damage is 31 before any reductions.

Titanic Wave

Evolution of: Breaking Fists. Prerequisite: having Breaking Fists. Type: active. Activation: 1 Bonus Action after a successful blow. Additional cost: 2 ST. Limit: once on your turn. To enlarge the area: Strength must have reached Prestige.

When, on your turn, an unarmed attack or a Striker offensive melee Technique hits a creature, object or surface, you may spend the Bonus Action and 2 ST to propagate the impact through the surrounding area. The maximum radius is that of your Strength Prestige; you may choose a smaller radius.

The Talent is the activation required to propagate the blow’s damage against creatures as well. The environmental scale alone remains available without the Talent and does not consume the Bonus Action or the 2 ST. To use Titanic Wave you must have the Talent, have the Bonus Action and at least 2 ST available, and have landed the blow on your turn. An attack that misses or is negated by a defence does not satisfy the condition. This wave requires neither Launch nor Crash.

The area originates at the point hit. The shock wave strikes the other creatures reached by the impact, excluding whoever generated it. Any allies in the area must defend normally. The Talent does not allow you to freely exclude individual creatures from the chosen area.

Titanic Wave is an exception granted by the Prestige Talent, not an Area Form in the Striker Builder. The Striker has no ordinary Style Area Techniques. The wave always comes from the propagation of a single successful blow and follows only its own radius, conditions and costs.

Any Area granted by a special Source remains separate and does not multiply origin points or increase Titanic Wave’s radius. Likewise, the wave does not enlarge or replace the Technique’s shape.

Use the original attack roll result to resolve the impact against each additional creature, applying Passive Defence, Active Defences, Reactions and cover according to ordinary rules. If the blow against a surface required no roll, make a single normal attack roll valid for the area. Each creature can therefore suffer a different outcome.

Roll the initial blow’s damage once, including Titan’s Power and other applicable bonuses. Use that result as D and, for each additional target hit, apply the fraction specified by their distance from the impact point. The original target has already received the blow: they do not suffer a second copy of the same damage merely because they are in the area.

Propagated damage does not include any subsequent Crash, and secondary effects aimed only at the initial target do not automatically extend to everyone. Techniques and Haki retain their own requirements, costs and limits.

Breaking Fists is incorporated into the evolution: unarmed blows continue to deal full damage to compatible objects. Full damage removes the penalties provided for hitting those objects unarmed; attenuation by distance from the impact continues to apply. Structures reached by the wave receive damage for their own band and give way according to their resilience and SP. This benefit does not automatically ignore creatures’ damage reductions.

Example: with Strength d20+d10 you can propagate a blow up to a 100 m radius. The initial blow deals 40 damage. A creature hit at 20 m receives 40 damage; at 40 m, 20; at 60 m, 10; at 90 m, 5, before their respective reductions. The initial target receives only one application of 40 damage.

Absolute Precision

Type: passive. Talent’s additional cost: 0 ST. Benefit: allows you to include Breaking Point in your own Striker offensive melee Techniques based on the Technique Attribute.

The Talent makes a precision effect available to choose when building the move. To apply it, the Technique must actually contain Breaking Point, have the necessary slot and pay its cost. Absolute Precision does not automatically apply to every basic attack.

Breaking Point

Family: Control. Cost: 2 ST. Slots: 1 Effect. Requirement: Absolute Precision and a Striker offensive melee Technique based on the Technique Attribute. Condition: a Clean Hit, with a margin of at least +4 according to ordinary rules.

Before the attack, declare the part or function you want to disable. The point must be accessible within the Technique’s reach and consistent with the target’s anatomy or the object’s operation. Defences, cover and the conditions needed to hit that target continue to apply.

Pay the effect’s cost together with the Technique’s other costs, even if the attack misses or does not achieve a Clean Hit. If you obtain only a hit, deal normal damage without applying Breaking Point.

On a Clean Hit you deal the Technique’s normal damage and may replace the Signature’s Stun or Off-Balance effect with one of the following results.

  • Hand: the struck hand cannot hold or use what it is carrying. The other hand and abilities that do not depend on the disabled hand remain available.
  • Leg: the target cannot perform voluntary Movement until they recover the function. They can still attack or use abilities compatible with their position.
  • Hold: you break a hold maintained with the struck part and free whoever or whatever that hold was restraining. The target can attempt a new hold under the ordinary rules.
  • Component: you damage an accessible element, such as a weapon’s trigger, the rudder, a joint or a cannon mechanism. Apply the Technique’s damage to that component, assessing its own resilience and Structure Points where present; the result depends on sufficient damage being dealt to compromise its function.

The limitation on a hand or leg ends when the target spends a main Action to recover the function, or at the end of combat. It represents temporary disabling, not permanent mutilation. A broken hold is an immediate result; damage to a component instead follows the rules for objects and repairs.

You can keep only one physical function disabled per target with this effect. A new application to a different part replaces the previous one; repeating the blow to the same part does not add further limitations.

Breaking Point replaces only the Signature’s Status for that blow and shares its limit of one activation per turn. Applying Breaking Point consumes the Signature’s sole activation; if Breakthrough Strike has already activated that turn, you cannot apply the Signature again through Breaking Point. Any other Technique effects follow their own rules, respecting the ordinary limit on effects per family. The Talent does not allow you to automatically ignore a defence or hit a physically unreachable part.

Example: you declare that you want to disable the hand an enemy is using to hold a pistol. The Technique with Breaking Point costs 2 ST for this effect, in addition to its other costs. You get a Clean Hit: the enemy suffers the damage, and the hand cannot use the pistol. They can recover the function by spending their main Action, or continue the battle using their remaining available abilities.

3.5Striker · Flurry

Endless Flurry

Endless Flurry belongs to the Striker’s shared catalogue. Acquiring it uses Athletics or Acrobatics, according to the chosen Role Skill, and one of the normal Prestige choices available.

Evolution of: Flurry · Mastery. Type: active. Activation: 1 Bonus Action after an unarmed attack. Limit: 1 activation on your turn. Progressive cost: 1 ST for the first extra attack, 2 ST for the second, 3 ST for the third, 4 ST for the fourth and so on, in addition to all the normal costs of any Technique.

After making an unarmed attack, you may activate Endless Flurry to perform a sequence of additional attacks on the same turn. The initial attack can be a basic attack or a Striker offensive melee Technique performed unarmed, and need not have hit.

Flurry’s cost increases for each additional attack made during that activation: the first costs 1 ST, the second 2 ST, the third 3 ST, the fourth 4 ST, the fifth 5 ST and so on. There is no predetermined maximum number of extra attacks: the limit is the Stamina the character can and chooses to spend. Each additional attack can be an unarmed basic attack or a Striker offensive melee Technique. If you choose a Technique, you always pay both Flurry’s progressive cost for that attack and all the normal costs of the Technique and its effects. Both costs are added in full.

Decide whether to continue after resolving each attack, and pay the next attack’s cost before attempting it. A missed attack consumes the paid Stamina normally, but does not interrupt Endless Flurry.

You may focus the attacks on the same opponent or distribute them among reachable targets. Each attack retains its own reach, attack roll, damage and the target’s normal defensive options. Endless Flurry grants no free Movement or special displacements. Any movements come from remaining available Movement or effects contained in the Technique used, and consume their costs and any budgets normally.

Additional attacks are not new main Actions and grant no further Bonus Actions. They cannot be used to activate other active Talents, activate Haki, perform Support or Enhancement Techniques or freely use a Special Move. Compatible passive Talents and upgrades can apply to individual blows according to their own rules, including Titan’s Power and the Attribute upgrades Colossal Launch, Lightning Displacement and Superhuman Agility. Every Technique must contain the necessary effects and respect their requirements.

This evolution fully incorporates the Flurry chain. On the same turn you cannot add or reactivate Flurry · Basic, Improved or Mastery to gain further additional attacks. Breakthrough Strike remains subject to the general limit of only one activation per turn.

You have 1 ST left and cannot pay for the third, which would cost 5 ST in total.

Example with mobility and precision: the first extra attack with a Technique containing Dash costs at least 2 ST, comprising 1 for Flurry and 1 for Dash, and consumes only the metres actually travelled from the Technique budget. The first extra attack with Breaking Point costs at least 3 ST, comprising 1 for Flurry and 2 for the effect, and requires Absolute Precision. Other effects are paid for normally.

3.6Swordsman with Athletics as a Role Skill

The four Talents are options to acquire through Athletics choices. Their physical scale depends on the Attribute specified by the blow; the roll added by Impassable Guard instead depends on the Athletics Skill.

Sovereign Slash

Evolution of: Air Slash. Type: passive. Additional cost per use: 1 ST. Use requirement: a compatible Swordsman offensive melee Technique performed with a blade.

When you use the Technique, you may unleash a slash that reaches the target within the scale’s reach. The cost of 1 ST is added to that of the Technique. It requires no separate Bonus Action.

Final damage remains full: Air Slash’s halving is removed. The Talent replaces its ordinary version and does not require paying for it a second time.

Make the Technique’s normal attack roll. The target uses their own defences and cover under the general rules. The slash travels through the space between you and the point hit; obstacles can intercept it and must be overcome or damaged under their own rules.

The Technique retains its own effects, slots and costs. A Single Technique continues to hit only one target. The Swordsman’s Close Area retains minimum Grade d8 and cost 2 ST: it cannot be projected at a distance through Sovereign Slash, remains centred on the character, and its radius equals the blade’s effective Reach. Ranged Area slashes require an ability that expressly grants them. Effects requiring physical contact retain that condition.

Example: you use a Technique based on the Technique Attribute d20+d10. You can reach an enemy up to 100 m away. The Technique costs 3 ST and its final damage is 28: with Sovereign Slash you pay 4 ST and, if you hit, deal 28 damage before the target’s reductions.

Colossal Cut

Type: active. Activation: 1 Bonus Action before the blow. Additional cost: 2 ST. Duration: the next attack with a blade on your turn. Benefit’s target: a single element of the environment.

Choose what you want to sever: a hull, a pillar, a wall, a mass of rock or a section of a larger structure. The chosen section must fit within the maximum size granted by the blow’s Attribute. The GM defines the element’s SP and any resistances before the roll.

You must be able to hit a useful point on that element with the attack’s normal reach. For a ranged cut you can use a Technique with Sovereign Slash, also paying its cost.

If the blow succeeds, damage against the chosen element is:

Structural damage = normal attack damage + one full roll of the Attribute used.

The additional roll is a damage roll separate from the attack roll. Roll it once, including the Prestige die, and add the results. With Strength d20+d8 you add d20+d8; with Technique Attribute d20+d12 you add d20+d12.

Apply the relevant resistances and subtract the damage received from the element’s SP. If the damage reduces its SP to zero, you sever it with that single slash. If SP remain, the element is damaged. For elements without SP, the GM determines the result using the normal rules for objects and materials.

Each use concerns one element and applies the damage only once. The maximum size indicates how large the cut can be; it does not multiply damage. Against a creature, this Talent does not add the structural bonus. Consequences of failure, such as a collapsing wall or an opening hull, are resolved separately.

The Bonus Action and 2 ST are spent before the attack and remain consumed if you miss. The upgrade ends after that blow or at the end of your turn.

Example: you choose a pillar with 40 SP, with a section within your scale. The blow deals 25 damage and the full Attribute roll adds 18. Structural damage is 43: if the pillar has no relevant reductions, you sever it. A pillar with 60 SP would instead be left at 17 SP.

Impassable Guard

Evolution of: Perfect Parry. Type: reactive. Additional cost: 2 ST and your Reaction. Use requirement: a Parry with a blade against an interceptable attack.

When you attempt the Parry, you may spend 2 ST to add a full Athletics roll to the normal defensive roll. With Athletics in Prestige, roll both dice and add the results.

Impassable Guard roll = Parry Attribute + Weapon Die + full Athletics roll.

Compare the roll with the attack’s result according to the general Parry rules. You can intercept a single attack that can physically be opposed with the blade, including large or area attacks. Defence compatibility and conditions required by the powers involved continue to apply.

A successful Parry protects you and allies within 5 m behind your position relative to the direction the blow comes from, provided your interception actually covers its trajectory towards them. A single roll resolves the defence against that attack.

You can therefore intercept a cannon shot before it reaches the crew or stop the front of a large slash. Separate attacks require separate defences and available Reactions. If the Parry fails, the blow is resolved normally against its targets.

You retain Perfect Parry’s retry: once per combat, after a failed Parry you may repeat the roll and must accept the second result. The retry repeats the full Impassable Guard roll, without spending its 2 ST again or using another Reaction.

The 2 ST are added to any costs of the defensive ability used. Impassable Guard uses the ordinary Reaction and does not require the Bonus Action. The Talent upgrades Parry and does not grant an attack in response.

Example: a cannon shot is resolved with an attack roll result of 38. Your normal Parry produces 24; the full Athletics roll produces 19. The total is 43: the Parry succeeds and also protects companions in the covered position behind you.

Absolute Blade Mastery

Type: passive. Additional cost: 0 ST. Application: any compatible blade you wield.

Any blade you wield uses d20 as its Weapon Die, regardless of its original Grade.

  • Basic attack: Attribute specified by the blade + d20.
  • Basic attack damage: 1d20, plus compatible benefits.
  • Parry: Attribute specified by the defence + d20.

A d4 knife, a d8 sabre and a d12 katana therefore use d20 for these rolls in your hands. The Talent always uses d20: Athletics Prestige unlocks its acquisition and does not add another die to the Weapon Die.

The blade retains its reach, properties and any special effects. The requirements for wielding it depend on its original Grade. The mastery belongs to the Swordsman: when another character wields that weapon, they use its normal Grade.

Swordsman Techniques continue to use their own Technique Die to hit and their own Damage Die. A d8 Technique remains d8 even when performed with a blade you use as d20. The Double Slash Signature follows the blow’s Damage Die: on a basic attack with Mastery it adds d20; on a Technique it adds the Damage Die specified by that move.

Absolute Blade Mastery can accompany Impassable Guard: use d20 as the Weapon Die in its formula and add the full Athletics roll only once.

Example: you wield a d4 knife associated with the Technique Attribute. With Technique Attribute d20+d8, the basic attack uses d20+d8+d20. If you hit, basic damage is 1d20. On a Clean Hit, the Signature adds another d20, for a total of 2d20 before other applicable benefits.

3.7Crusher with Athletics as a Role Skill

A Crusher built on Strength uses Attribute Prestige for environmental destruction and Colossal Launch. Athletics instead grants the choices to acquire the following four Talents. The three ordinary acquisitions at d20+d4, d20+d8 and d20+d12 allow you to choose three options from the catalogue, respecting their prerequisites; they do not grant all four Talents.

The Talents require bludgeoning weapons compatible with the Role and the Arsenal rules. The attack roll retains the Attribute specified by the weapon or Technique, without freely changing it. Strength determines the scale when referenced and its own roll when an ability requires it; Athletics unlocks choices and provides the additional roll specified by Colossus Stop. Growth in one progression does not automatically increase the other.

The Crusher’s ordinary Signature is called Devastating Impact: on a Clean Hit it pushes the target 3 m, with no additional ST. It is separate from the Striker’s Titanic Wave Prestige Talent. The Crusher’s Signature does not grant an offensive area wave and does not replace the Launch effect required for Colossal Launch. The Crusher does not receive the Technique Prestige mobility package.

Unstoppable Maul

Evolution of: Heavy Strike Mastery. Prerequisite: having Heavy Strike Mastery. Type: active, blow modifier. Activation: declaration before the attack, without an additional Bonus Action. Cost: 3 ST in total instead of Mastery’s 2 ST. Limit: once on your turn.

You may accompany a Crusher basic attack or offensive melee Technique performed with a compatible bludgeoning weapon. Pay the 3 ST on declaration, in addition to any Technique costs. This version replaces Heavy Strike Mastery and retains Advantage on the attack roll, +2 Damage Dice and the target becoming Off-Balance if the blow hits.

Your PD decreases by 5 from declaration until the start of your next turn. The penalty and cost remain even if the blow misses, is dodged or is parried. The Basic, Improved, Mastery and Unstoppable Maul versions do not stack to repeat bonuses or penalties.

In addition, separately roll Strength once, including any Prestige die, and add the result to the blow’s damage. The additional roll concerns only this attack and does not modify the attack roll.

Unstoppable Maul damage = Heavy Strike Mastery damage + one full Strength roll.

If the attack exceeds the defence required to hit but the target stops it with a successful Parry, they still suffer half of the total damage, rounded down, then apply their own reductions. The Parry holds back the weapon but transmits part of the impact.

Residual damage from a parried blow does not apply Off-Balance, the Signature, Launch, Crash or other blow effects. It does not count as a successful blow to activate Seismic Wave, Shattered Guard or Guard Breaker. Other defences retain their own rules: a successful Dodge avoids the attack, and a roll that does not exceed the defence required to hit deals no damage, even halved.

If the blow instead hits normally, full damage and compatible effects apply. For a subsequent valid propagation with Seismic Wave, D also includes this additional Strength roll. Any Crash uses its own separate Strength roll and does not receive Unstoppable Maul’s bonus again.

Example: Mastery damage is 30 and the additional Strength roll gives 18. Damage becomes 48 before reductions. If the blow hits, the target receives 48 damage and becomes Off-Balance; if they parry it successfully after the attack has exceeded the required defence, they receive 24 damage before reductions, and other effects are stopped. In both cases the Crusher has spent 3 ST and retains PD −5 for the specified duration.

Seismic Wave

Evolution of: Chain Impact. Prerequisite: having Chain Impact. Type: active. Activation: 1 Bonus Action after a successful blow on your turn. Cost: 3 ST in total instead of Shock Wave’s 2 ST. Limit: once on your turn. For this wave: Strength of at least d20+d4 and a valid impact on a surface.

When a Strength-based bludgeoning attack of yours hits the ground, an object, a structure or a creature and the impact is transmitted to a surface, you may spend the Bonus Action and 3 ST to also attack creatures resting on that surface. It can be a basic attack or a compatible offensive Technique. A blow that misses, is dodged or is parried does not allow activation.

The destruction scale alone already allows you to damage the environment without these costs. Seismic Wave adds direct damage, push and Off-Balance against creatures: it uses Strength’s existing radius and does not create a second destruction scale.

The wave starts at the impact point and travels in a 90° cone. When you activate it, declare the direction and chosen length, up to your Strength’s maximum radius. For example, with Strength d20+d10 the cone can extend up to 100 m; at Saikyō, up to 1,000 m. Damage bands remain calculated on Strength’s maximum radius, even if you choose a shorter cone.

Seismic Wave always originates from a single valid impact and follows its own Prestige shape. The Crusher’s Close Area, if present in the Technique, retains minimum Grade d8 and cost 2 ST, remains centred on the character and has a radius equal to the weapon’s effective Reach: it does not generate several cones, create new centres for Seismic Wave or increase its radius.

The single point where the bludgeoning impact is delivered to the surface must be identifiable: the entire wave is resolved from that point. A larger Area granted by a special Source remains separate; Area and Prestige cone do not increase one another’s dimensions.

Only creatures resting on the ground or the physically connected surface reached by the wave are involved. Being above the cone while flying or suspended without contact is not enough. An obstacle that withstands the impact can stop propagation, and a separate surface is not automatically reached. The Crusher generating the wave is excluded; allies in the zone can be hit and cannot be freely excluded.

Use the original attack roll result against each additional creature, resolving PD, Active Defences, Reactions and cover according to normal rules. If the blow against a surface required no roll, make a single normal attack roll valid for the wave. Each creature can suffer a different outcome.

Roll the initial damage once and use it as D, including Unstoppable Maul and other bonuses applicable to the blow, but excluding any subsequent Crash. Each additional creature hit receives the fraction specified by distance, then applies their own reductions; they are also pushed 3 m away from the impact and made Off-Balance, unless relevant protections apply. An existing Status does not stack with a second copy of the same Status.

The initial target has already received the blow and does not receive a second copy of damage, push or Off-Balance from this wave. Other Technique effects and the Signature do not extend to additional targets. Structures reached receive only one application of their band’s damage: the passive scale and the Talent describe the same impact.

The cone replaces Chain Impact’s limit of three additional enemies within 3 m. You do not also pay the previous version’s 2 ST and do not add a second wave through Shock Wave. Seismic Wave makes Chain Impact unavailable as a separate choice; its benefits are incorporated into the evolution.

The wave’s push remains 3 m: it does not become a 100 m launch and does not automatically generate Crash. Colossal Launch and its collision damage concern the target of the Technique actually containing Launch and, for damage, Crash.

Example: with Strength d20+d10 you hit the ground and initial damage is 40. Without the Talent you can damage the environment within the chosen radius, up to 100 m. If you activate Seismic Wave by paying the Bonus Action and 3 ST, a creature hit at 20 m in the cone receives 40 damage; at 40 m, 20; at 60 m, 10; at 90 m, 5, before reductions. Hit creatures are also pushed 3 m and made Off-Balance if they lack relevant protections.

Shattered Guard

Evolution of: Guard Breaker. Prerequisite: having Guard Breaker. Type: passive. Additional cost: 0 ST. Additional action: none. Limit: 1 application on your turn.

When a direct attack of yours with a compatible bludgeoning weapon achieves a Clean Hit against a creature, with the normal margin of at least +4, you can open their guard completely. The target suffers the blow’s normal damage.

Until the end of their next turn, they cannot Parry or use Active Defences based on physically blocking the blow. They can still dodge, move away and use defences that avoid the attack; they retain their PD and damage reductions. A defensive Technique can be used only if the way it avoids the attack is compatible with this limitation.

The effect represents the weapon being knocked aside and the defensive stance being compromised. It does not automatically destroy the weapon or shield and does not deactivate Haki, a Fruit or the target’s reductions. Any damage to an object must be produced and resolved under the appropriate rules.

If the chosen blow hits without being Clean, instead apply normal Guard Breaker: the target’s next Parry or Active Defence, before the end of their next turn, is made with Disadvantage. Both versions share this use and do not produce two penalties through the same blow. A new application does not stack further prohibitions or simultaneous durations.

An attack that misses, is avoided or is parried does not activate the Talent. Residual damage from Unstoppable Maul after a successful Parry does not open the guard. Seismic Wave does not distribute Shattered Guard to all creatures reached: the effect concerns the direct target of the chosen blow.

Example: on your turn you hit an enemy fighting with a shield with a Clean margin. Until the end of their next turn, they cannot stop blows with the shield or a Parry, but can dodge and retain their other compatible defences. The shield remains intact until it suffers sufficient structural damage to break it.

Colossus Stop

Evolution of: Interception Blow. Prerequisites: having Interception Blow and Absolute Centre of Gravity. Type: reactive. Activation: 1 Reaction when an enemy enters your weapon’s reach during movement. Cost: 3 ST. Limit: once per turn, using the available Reaction.

You may use the Talent only while satisfying all the conditions of Absolute Centre of Gravity: you are conscious, in contact with the ground, and not moving voluntarily. You must wield a compatible bludgeoning weapon and be able to reach the enemy as they enter its normal reach. The destruction scale does not extend interception reach.

Before the enemy continues moving beyond that point, spend the Reaction and 3 ST and make a bludgeoning basic attack. Add a full Athletics roll, including any Prestige die, to the normal attack roll. Athletics is added to the attack roll, not to damage.

Colossus Stop roll = normal basic attack roll + one full Athletics roll.

The enemy has their normal defences available. If the blow hits and deals actual damage, their movement ends immediately and cannot resume for the rest of that turn. A larger size does not negate this consequence: even an enormous creature can be stopped if the attack hits and damages them.

In this way you can interrupt a charge, dash or leap when the enemy actually passes through the weapon’s reach. If you interrupt a leap, falls and the need for a foothold are resolved normally. Movement that does not pass through a position reachable by the weapon does not trigger interception.

The target retains their remaining actions and can use them from the position where they were stopped. The effect stops movement and does not automatically apply Stun or cancel all their actions. A subsequent push or other forced displacement follows its own rules.

If you miss, the enemy avoids the blow or their reductions bring actual damage to zero, movement is not stopped. The 3 ST and Reaction remain spent. The Talent uses the same interception as the previous version, without adding a second attack or a second Reaction. Colossus Stop makes Interception Blow unavailable as a separate choice.

The attack retains normal basic damage and effects compatible with the Role’s basic attack. It does not become a Technique or acquire Launch or Crash for free. Unstoppable Maul requires use on your turn; Seismic Wave also requires your Bonus Action and its own conditions. These Talents are not automatically added to an interception on other turns.

Example: you remain planted on the ground and a giant enters your club’s reach during a charge. You spend 3 ST and the Reaction, add Athletics to the normal basic attack, and hit. If the giant suffers damage after reductions, they stop at that point and cannot continue moving that turn, but can still perform actions possible from that position.

3.8Sniper with Observation as a Role Skill

The four Talents are options to acquire through the three choices available at Observation d20+d4, d20+d8 and d20+d12. They are not all granted automatically. Sniper Talents require ranged attacks performed with weapons compatible with the Style and do not transfer to melee blows.

Cunning and Observation remain independent: Cunning determines range and deviation scales and its own rolls when required; Observation unlocks choices. Having a Talent does not automatically bring Cunning into Prestige, and reaching Cunning Prestige does not grant Talents.

Decisive Shot

Evolution of: Held Breath. Prerequisite: having Held Breath. Type: active. Activation: 1 Bonus Action before the shot. Additional cost: 2 ST. Limit: once on your turn.

You prepare the next ranged attack against a single target, to be made by the end of your turn. It can be a basic attack or a compatible offensive Technique. If the blow hits, add a full Cunning roll, including the Prestige die, to damage, and the blow is considered Clean for the Signature and other compatible effects requiring that result.

Decisive Shot damage = ordinary blow damage + one full Cunning roll.

The target retains their defences. Clean Hit qualification applies only after a successful attack: it does not change the attack roll or turn a missed attack into a success. Total damage undergoes the target’s normal reductions.

The benefit is consumed by the next valid attack, even if it misses; if you do not make it by the end of the turn, the benefit ends. The 2 ST are spent on activation and are added to any Technique costs. The Talent concerns a single blow against a single target and is not distributed through area attacks, Ricochet or Chain.

The evolution uses this activation and replaces Held Breath’s limit with one use on your turn. Both versions do not stack to multiply the benefit. The additional Cunning roll applies only once.

Example: with Cunning d20+d8, a blow normally deals 12 damage. You roll Cunning separately and get 19: damage becomes 31 before reductions. Since the blow succeeded, it is also considered Clean and can activate Exposed Point or Hawk’s Eye, if acquired.

Surgical Precision

Evolution of: Targeted Shot. Prerequisite: having Targeted Shot. Type: modifier to a ranged attack against a single target. Activation: declaration before the shot, without a Bonus Action. Additional cost: 2 ST.

You choose a precise point and declare the consequence you want to produce. Make the normal attack against the target’s defences. If you get a Clean Hit, deal ordinary damage and also apply the targeted consequence, instead of giving up damage as with Targeted Shot.

  • Hand: the target cannot use that hand until the end of their next turn.
  • Leg: the target cannot perform voluntary Movement until the end of their next turn.
  • Object or component: focus the damage on the chosen element; if it reaches 0 SP, you break it or interrupt its operation.

The chosen point must be reachable by the trajectory. A hand or leg is temporarily disabled: this effect does not automatically cause mutilation. Only one limb disabling can be active on the same target; a new application replaces the previous one.

If you hit without getting a Clean Hit, deal only ordinary damage. If you miss, deal no damage and apply no consequence. The 2 ST are paid even if the shot misses or fails to meet the requirement. The costs of the attack or Technique used remain.

When you hit an object, damage applies to the component actually chosen, with its own SP and resilience. You do not also deal the same damage to the creature holding it or to the entire structure. The evolution’s effect does not stack with a second free consequence from Targeted Shot on the same roll.

Example: an officer is about to use a cannon. You strike their hand with a Clean Hit: deal normal damage to the officer and prevent them from using that hand until the end of their next turn. If you instead choose the cannon’s mechanism, damage applies to that component and stops it only if it brings it to 0 SP.

Sovereign Interdiction

Evolution of: Anticipated Shot. Prerequisite: having Anticipated Shot. Type: reactive. Activation: 1 Reaction when an observed enemy declares an Action, before they complete it. Additional cost: 2 ST.

You attack the enemy before they complete the declared Action, with a valid ranged attack and respecting range, path and weapon availability. If the shot hits, deal normal damage, and the opponent’s Action occurs with Disadvantage, as with Anticipated Shot.

If you get a Clean Hit, you may instead prevent that Action, provided the shot concretely interrupts what is needed to perform it. You can hit the hand before a shot, break the control before activation or interrupt the gesture needed for a Technique. The point and how the blow obstructs the manoeuvre must be declared before the shot. If interruption requires breaking a component, its SP and resilience apply.

The opponent consumes the declared Action and retains the turn’s other resources and possibilities. Resources already spent on that Action remain spent. A Clean Hit that cannot physically interrupt the manoeuvre applies normal Disadvantage without cancelling it.

The Talent concerns a manoeuvre not yet completed: a projectile already fired follows its own resolution normally. A missed attack neither penalises nor interrupts the enemy’s Action. The Reaction and 2 ST are spent anyway; any Technique also pays its own costs.

The evolution uses the same Reaction as the anticipated shot and grants no second Reaction or other attack through Anticipated Shot. You may apply it only if the required Reaction is available.

Example: you observe an enemy shooter declaring a shot at an ally. You spend the Reaction and 2 ST and attack the hand holding the weapon. If you hit without a Clean Hit, their shot is made with Disadvantage; if you get a Clean Hit and the manoeuvre can be interrupted in this way, the enemy consumes the Action without completing the shot.

Bullet Dance

Type: passive modifier of Sniper Techniques with Ricochet or Chain. Additional cost: 1 ST per use, in addition to Technique costs. Activation: no additional Bonus Action. Use requirement: a compatible Technique containing one of these two effects, with the corresponding slot.

When you use a Sniper Technique with Ricochet or Chain, every target reached receives the move’s full damage, replacing the progressive reduction specified by the effect.

  • Ricochet: up to two targets, respecting the ordinary limit of 5 m between the first and second.
  • Chain: up to three targets arranged in a line, respecting the effect’s other requirements.

Make a separate attack roll against each target and roll damage once. Each target applies defences and reductions normally. A missed target receives no damage, and no target can be hit twice by the same sequence.

The Talent modifies the damage of effects already present in the Technique; it grants no new slots or larger number of targets. Ricochet and Chain remain alternatives from the same family and are not added together to increase the number of blows.

If the path requires unusual trajectories, also use Impossible Shot, paying its 2 ST and respecting the number of deviations allowed by Cunning. Shot Geometry can modify the path, but does not remove the distances and arrangement required by Ricochet or Chain.

Example: a Technique with Chain hits three enemies in a line and the rolled damage is 18. With Bullet Dance, every enemy hit receives 18 damage before their own reductions, instead of 18, 9 and 4. If the Technique’s only ordinary cost is Chain at 2 ST, use costs 3 ST in total: 2 for Chain and 1 for the Talent.

4.5Crusher combinations

If the opponent successfully parries Maul, they receive only the residual damage specified by the Talent. That result does not allow Seismic Wave activation and does not apply Shattered Guard. A compatible Crusher Technique with Launch and Crash can accompany Maul. Initial damage includes Maul’s bonus; Crash is a separate full Strength roll, applied only to the recipients of the first collision. You do not copy all initial damage onto the obstacle or add a second Maul bonus to the collision.

For Seismic Wave, the centre and positions involved are those at the moment of the initial impact. If the blow also produces a launch, the wave retains that centre; any subsequent Crash does not become a second centre of the same wave and does not increase D. A Crusher Technique with Close Area retains minimum Grade d8 and cost 2 ST, keeps the Area centred on the Crusher and uses the weapon’s effective Reach as its radius. Larger areas require a special Source and remain separate from the Prestige wave.

The Crusher’s ordinary Signature pushes 3 m on a Clean Hit. When the same Technique applies Colossal Launch to the same target, resolve the chosen launch according to that effect without adding a second launch for free through the Signature. The 3 m push on Seismic Wave’s additional targets remains separate from the initial target’s launch.

Seismic Wave requires the sole Bonus Action on your turn. If activating Haki requires that action, you cannot also use it for the wave on the same turn; an already active Colour can accompany compatible blows according to its own rules. The passive environmental scale does not consume this action.

Colossus Stop uses the Reaction. You can use it even after spending the Bonus Action if you have the Reaction, 3 ST and all the conditions of Absolute Centre of Gravity. 4.6Sniper combinations

Decisive Shot and Surgical Precision can accompany the same attack against a single target. Before the shot, spend the Bonus Action and 2 ST for Decisive Shot and declare the targeted point, paying another 2 ST for Surgical Precision. The attack retains its own defences and any Technique costs. If it hits, add the Cunning roll to damage, and the blow is considered Clean, also satisfying the requirement for the targeted consequence.

Example: the shot deals 12 ordinary damage, and the additional Cunning roll gives 19. A successful blow against the hand deals 31 damage before reductions and prevents use of that hand until the end of the target’s next turn. The two Talents cost 4 ST; if Impossible Shot is also needed, the total rises to 6 ST, in addition to any Technique costs.

Decisive Shot requires the turn’s sole Bonus Action. On the same turn, you cannot also use it for Rapid Reload or activate Haki requiring that action. An already active Colour can accompany compatible attacks according to its own rules.

Sovereign Interdiction uses the Reaction and can be used after spending the Bonus Action on your turn if you have the required Reaction and Stamina. Surgical Precision can modify that shot by paying another 2 ST and declaring the chosen point beforehand; Decisive Shot instead prepares the next shot by the end of your turn and does not wait for a Reaction on other turns.

Bullet Dance concerns Techniques with several targets and does not accompany Decisive Shot or Surgical Precision, which require a single target. Impossible Shot and Shot Geometry can accompany the sequence when the path is valid and all costs are paid. The spatial requirements of Ricochet or Chain remain necessary.

22.4Ryou and a Role Talent

With Armament d20+d12 already active, spend 4 PIP and the Bonus Action for Persistent Ryou. Choose your physical attack for the turn and resolve it with Ryou according to ordinary rules.

On the next turn Ryou is still active and can accompany another physical attack. You may use the Bonus Action for a compatible Role Talent, paying its costs and respecting its conditions. At the start of the second turn after activation, Persistent Ryou ends.

22.5Pressure and Coating are two separate choices

Imperial Pressure uses the Bonus Action to interrupt and block a target’s Haki at range. Sovereign Impact and Unreadable Will use the Bonus Action to prepare your coated attack. Both cannot be declared on the same turn with the normal availability of one Bonus Action.

Pressure’s block remains valid for its duration even if you choose another effect on a later turn. Unreadable Will instead excludes active Observation defence only against the single coated attack.

22.7Interaction with Techniques, Talents and Ultimate

These upgrades modify the use of Spirit and active Haki effects. They do not automatically grant a Technique, new Role Talent, new Colour or innate Conqueror’s ability.

Techniques retain ordinary reach, targets, slots and costs, unless a modification is expressly described by the effect used. Role Skill Prestige Talents and benefits of other Attributes remain in their respective paths.

No Prestige evolution of Ultimates is introduced for any Role. Their operation and requirements remain those already defined.
