Grand Line Chronicles · Guidelines for Mechanical Body and Modules

A Cyborg does not simply carry tools: they integrate them into their body. Mechanical Body establishes how many Modules it can house and what technological level it can support; Modules define what that technology can do.

This manual gathers the rules needed to design clear, comparable Modules that can be used at the table, without turning every technological description into an unlimited collection of bonuses.

Chapter 1 ·

Being a Cyborg

A Cyborg is an individual whose body has been modified through prosthetics, implants and integrated mechanical devices. Two Cyborgs of the same Grade can have completely different bodies: one could be a weapons platform, while another focuses on mobility, sensors or tools.

1.1 · Mechanical Body

Every Cyborg possesses the Racial Technique Mechanical Body. Its Grade represents the quality and level of integration of the technology in the body, not the character's amount of metal or mass.

Mechanical Body determines two things: the number of available slots and the maximum Technology Tier of installable Modules.

1.2 · Modules

A Module is a technological device integrated into the body and recorded as part of Mechanical Body. It can be an artificial limb, a weapon, a thruster, armour, a sensor, a tool or another consistent system.

Every Module must have a concrete function. Calling a device “advanced” grants no benefits: what it is designed to do must be defined.

1.3 · Modules and Techniques

A Module is not a Technique. The Module is the device; the Technique is the particular way the character uses their abilities.

A Module can be used according to its function or as the means of executing a compatible Technique. It does not automatically grant Signature Move Builder Effects, roll bonuses, Statuses, additional damage or other properties not specified in its sheet.

1.4 · General principle

The function states what a Module can do; the Technology Tier establishes how much it can do. Special Effects introduce any exceptions. Slots, energy, activation and integrity establish when the device can be used.

The narrative description cannot freely increase power, distance or autonomy. At the same time, the rules define limits and values without imposing the same arsenal on every Cyborg.

Chapter 2 ·

Mechanical Body

Mechanical Body measures the Cyborg's level of technological integration. Its ordinary progression is d6 → d8 → d10 → d12 → d20 and it has no Prestige progression.

A Cyborg can install Modules of their own Technology Tier or a lower Tier. Having a free slot does not allow a device beyond their Mechanical Body Grade to be installed.

2.2 · Slots and Technology Tier

Slots measure how many Modules can be integrated; Technology Tier measures how advanced each Module can be.

Every installed Module normally occupies 1 slot, regardless of Tier.

A slot does not necessarily correspond to an anatomical part. Multiple Modules can occupy the same part of the body if the configuration is plausible, but independent technological systems remain separate Modules.

2.3 · Technology Tier and Weapon Grade

Technology Tier indicates the Mechanical Body requirement. Weapon Grade instead indicates the die used by a Weapon Module. The two values are separate: a Tier d10 cannon could, for example, be a d8 weapon.

2.4 · Growth

When Mechanical Body increases in Grade, the Cyborg gains the new slot specified by the table and can install Modules of the new Tier. They do not receive a new Module for free.

Modules already owned do not automatically increase in Tier. To improve them, they must be modified, rebuilt or replaced through the specified procedures.

2.5 · Independent values

Mechanical Body is independent of the Strength Attribute. Increasing Mechanical Body does not increase Strength, Passive Defence, unarmed damage or general lifting capacity. Increasing Strength does not grant slots or access to higher Tiers.

The ordinary limit is d20: 5 slots and Modules up to Tier d20. Any exceptions must come from a specific ability.

3.2 · Installation, integrity and activation

A Module can exist without being installed. Only an installed Module occupies a slot and becomes part of the Mechanical Body system.

An Intact Module is functional; a Damaged Module cannot use its Operational Function, Special Effect or Techniques that require it until repaired.

An installed, intact Module can be Active or Inactive. Being installed, being intact and being active are three distinct conditions.

3.3 · Inactive function and active function

The Inactive function includes only physical properties not requiring the system's operational functioning: for example, a compartment that continues to hold objects or an artificial limb retaining normal mobility if the design provides for it.

Propulsion, advanced sensors, defensive bonuses, Special Effects and linked Techniques do not automatically remain active. If the sheet specifies no Inactive function, an inactive Module grants no mechanical benefits.

The Active function instead describes the device's normal functioning when installed, intact and active.

3.4 · Effects, energy and limits

A Module can have at most one Special Effect. If it uses a dedicated resource, the sheet specifies capacity, consumption and recharging. Compatible Techniques and Limits specify any further conditions without granting additional benefits.

Chapter 4 ·

Technology Tiers

Technology Tier establishes the maximum limit of the Module's Main Function. It does not automatically grant every capability in the same row: each device uses only the parameters needed for its function.

4.3 · Protection

TierProtected scale
d6Part of the body or a limited direction
d8The entire user
d10User + 1 adjacent target or a small barrier
d12Small group or extended barrier
d20Group or broad defensive position

The Tier establishes the scale of protection, not the damage cancelled: the specific benefit depends on the chosen Defensive Function.

4.4 · Tier limits

The tables represent normal operating conditions. Temporarily exceeding a load or operating in extreme conditions may require an appropriate Check, but does not permanently turn a Module into a higher Tier.

No Tier automatically grants Builder effects such as Grapple, Launch, Statuses, Areas, Ranges or other combat results. Tier describes only the device's material capability. Combat effects come from specific Functions, Special Effects, Weapon Modules, Techniques or Talents. Areas and Persistent Zones require a function or design expressly producing them. The ordinary Builder no longer grants additional damage dice as a purchasable effect.

A Module has one Main Function. Secondary components necessary for it to function are allowed, but independent mechanical abilities require a compatible Special Effect or a second Module.

Chapter 5 ·

Module Functions

The Main Function is what the Module can normally do. A continuous Function operates while the Module is active; an operational Function normally requires 1 Action unless its description states otherwise.

5.1 · Offensive

A Module directly dealing damage must be a Weapon Module or use a Technique or ability expressly assigning damage or Statuses.

A Weapon Module states whether it is Melee or Ranged and has a Weapon Grade. Technology Tier establishes whether the body can support it; Weapon Grade determines its offensive effectiveness.

5.2 · Defensive

The two main Defensive Functions are Armour and Shield/Barrier.

5.2.1 · Armour

TierPhysical damage reduction

d12−4

Reduction applies after any halving and cannot take damage below 0. Specific resistances to fire, cold, electricity, acid, poison, Haki or other damage require a property provided by the design.

5.2.2 · Shield or Barrier

When correctly facing the threat, grants Partial Cover: PD +2. Tier increases the protected scale, not the bonus.

TierProtection

Partial Cover does not stack with another identical source; the best benefit is used. Full Cover requires a specific ability.

5.3 · Mobility

A Thruster uses the Displacement of its Tier once per turn. This Displacement adds to normal Movement and does not constitute an additional Action. Available flight capabilities depend on Tier according to Chapter 4. Any energy consumption is paid normally.

5.4 · Control

Manipulators, cables, harpoons and similar systems use their Tier's Functional reach and Mechanical strength to grasp, support, move or operate objects.

Against a creature, physically reaching it does not automatically grant Grapple, Immobilised, Attraction, Launch or other forced displacement. These results require a normal appropriate check, a Technique with an effect compatible with its Style, or a Module Function or Special Effect expressly granting them.

An anchoring system can support the Cyborg, pull objects within its load capacity or allow an anchored point to be reached, always within the device's Reach.

5.5 · Utility

Utility Modules include sensors, integrated tools, and analysis, communication, lighting and storage systems.

A Sensor is designed for a defined type of information and uses the radius specified by its Tier. It does not automatically detect everything within that radius: concealment, interference and environmental conditions may require a Check.

TierIntegrated tool
d6Single common implement or tool
d8Small professional set
d10Advanced precision equipment
d12Portable specialised technical workstation
d20Mobile laboratory specialising in a single field

An Integrated tool replaces the necessary equipment, but does not automatically increase the Skill, provide materials or grant professional Talents.

5.6 · Emissions

A Module can emit light, smoke, foam, steam, signals, liquids or other elements within its Tier's Functional area. Emission alone does not automatically inflict damage, Statuses, Advantage or Disadvantage: a rule granting a combat effect is needed to obtain one.

5.7 · Creative use

A player can propose creative uses consistent with the Function. The GM can permit them directly or require a Check. Creative use does not turn the Module into a Function it does not possess or automatically duplicate effects already supplied by Techniques or Special Effects.

Chapter 6 ·

Special Effects

A Special Effect is an additional property of the Module. It is optional, and each Module can have at most one. A single Effect grants only one main mechanical benefit: conditions and limits can specify it, but cannot conceal multiple independent advantages.

The Special Effect belongs to the same Technology Tier as the Module and must be consistent with its functioning.

6.1 · Discount

Reduces the cost of a specific ability used through the Module. It must precisely identify the Technique, Builder Effect or ability concerned.

TierDiscount Points
d61

  • 1 Discount Point = −1 ST. The cost cannot fall below 1 ST.
  • From d8: 2 Discount Points = −1 Effect Slot. A Slot can decrease to 0.
  • The Discount does not grant the ability, remove requirements or apply outside the Module.
  • Multiple Discounts on the same cost do not automatically stack: use the best.

6.2 · Unlock

Makes one Signature Move Builder Effect materially enabled by the technology available through the Module.

The unlocked Effect cannot have a minimum Grade above the Module's Tier. Unlock does not reduce the minimum Grade or remove Form, Slots, ST, Talent prerequisites, Haki, Fruits, Ultimate, Prestige or fundamental limits of the Role Manuals.

6.3 · Passive

A Passive works while the Module is installed, intact and active. The main models are:

6.4 · Reaction

Allows a Module property to be used in response to a specific Trigger. The sheet must state the Trigger, effect, frequency and any costs. The Reaction does not grant additional Reactions.

TierMaximum frequency

The Reaction uses only one Module Function and normally pays Energy, Charges, ST or Ammunition. From d12, it can use a Function that would normally require an Action, provided the Trigger is specific. At d20, it can include the Module's Emergency Activation as part of the same Reaction.

6.5 · Curse

A Curse expands a Discount, Unlock, Passive or Reaction in only one aspect, but imposes a real mechanical consequence. It can represent overload, instability, overheating or experimental technology.

  • Discount: can extend to a small, consistent family of effects.
  • Unlock: can include 2 closely linked Effects.
  • Passive: can extend the benefit to two closely related conditions.
  • Reaction: can increase by 1 use per combat, without exceeding the normal Reaction economy.

Possible prices of the Curse:

  • Doubled consumption.
  • +1 ST per use.
  • Overheating: the Module becomes Inactive until the start of the next turn.
  • Overload damage according to Tier.
  • Specific operational vulnerability.

The consequence must be truly relevant. A Curse does not allow Tier d20 to be exceeded.

6.6 · Common rules

  • Unless explicitly excepted, a Special Effect requires the Module to be Installed, Intact and Active.
  • If it modifies a Technique, the Technique must be executed through that Module.
  • Identical effects do not automatically multiply: two sources of Advantage remain a single Advantage; two identical Unlocks give no further benefits.
  • No normal Special Effect grants additional Actions, additional Reactions, Ultimate, Haki, Prestige, Devil Fruits, Role Talents, general immunity or infinite resources.
Chapter 7 ·

Energy and Charges

Not all Modules require a separate resource. Each sheet specifies Energy: None or a consistent source such as a battery, fuel, pressure, ammunition or other Charges. Energy limits uses that actually require it, not every normal movement of the device.

7.1 · Charges

TierMaximum Charges
d63
d84
d105
d126
d208

A normal use consumes 1 Charge. Particularly intense Functions can specify higher consumption or an upkeep cost; consumption must appear in the sheet.

7.2 · Charges and Stamina

Charges and Stamina are different resources. If a Technique costs ST and the Module executing it consumes Charges, both are paid. Charges do not replace the Technique's cost.

7.3 · Recharging

Charges are not automatically recovered through Rest. If the necessary resource is available, a normal full recharge requires 10 minutes outside combat. Ammunition, special fuels or rare resources must be owned, purchased or produced according to the design.

7.4 · Common sources

An easy-to-find source does not make the Module infinite. A 5-Charge tank remains a 5-Charge tank even if it uses water, air, coal or electricity and the source is immediately available.

7.5 · Modules without Charges

A Module with Energy: None can normally use its Function. Actions, ST, limits, activation and any Special Effects specified by the sheet still apply.

Chapter 8 ·

Weapon Modules and Techniques

A Module built to deal damage directly can be classified as a Weapon Module and has both a Technology Tier and a Weapon Grade.

8.1 · Weapon Grade

Weapon Grade follows the scale d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12 → d20 and cannot exceed the Module's Technology Tier.

Example: d10 Module · d8 Weapon is valid; d8 Module · d10 Weapon is not.

8.2 · Basic attack

When a Weapon Module makes a normal attack, it uses:

Attack roll = Attribute + Weapon Grade

Damage = Weapon Die

Range, ammunition and special conditions depend on the design or Catalogue entry.

8.3 · The Module as the means of executing a Technique

A Module can execute a compatible Technique, but does not replace the Technique Die. The Technique normally uses Attribute + Technique Die and its own Damage Die. The Module provides the physical means, its Function, any Special Effect and any Charge costs.

8.4 · Grade and compatibility

The Technique's Grade still depends on the Attribute used and does not increase because the Module has a higher Tier. The Technique must also be physically compatible with the device and meet Form, Slots, ST, requirements and any Role limitations.

8.5 · No duplication

When a Technique uses a property already made possible by the Module, that property is the means of the action and is not applied twice. A telescopic arm allowing a target to be reached does not add a second Attraction to a Technique that already has Attraction.

Chapter 9 ·

Module Activation

The Cyborg can have multiple Modules installed, but normally keeps only one Active Module at a time.

9.1 · Active Module

The Active Module can use its Active function, Special Effect and linked Techniques. The other installed Modules remain Inactive and retain only any Inactive functions specified in their sheets.

9.2 · Initial Module

Outside combat, the Cyborg freely chooses which Module to keep active. At the start of a battle they retain the one already active; if none was declared, they choose one without spending Actions.

9.3 · Switch Module

Once during their turn, the Cyborg can switch their Active Module for free. Switching uses no Action, Bonus Action, Movement or Reaction.

The new Module immediately becomes Active and the previous one Inactive. A second free switch in the same turn is not possible.

9.4 · Duration and shutdown

A Module remains Active until replaced, voluntarily shut down, Damaged, deprived of its necessary resource or deactivated by a specific ability. It does not automatically shut down at the end of a turn or battle.

Voluntarily shutting down a Module during your turn is free, but does not grant a second switch in the same turn.

9.5 · Outside your turn

Normally, you cannot switch Modules outside your turn. An Effect or ability with Emergency Activation can do so through a Reaction; the new Module immediately replaces the previous one.

Chapter 10 ·

Construction, Installation and Integrity

A Module can be built by the crew, commissioned from a professional or obtained during the adventure. Technology Tier determines its construction difficulty; function, materials and appearance remain linked to the individual design.

10.1 · Building a Module

Construction normally uses Cunning + Mechanics. The Module's Tier establishes the minimum Mechanics Grade, Threshold and base work time.

The table provides reference values. A particularly simple, bulky, experimental or unconventional design can be modified by the GM without changing the Module's Technology Tier.

10.2 · Materials and design freedom

There is no universal list of mandatory materials for each Tier. Materials vary by Module and are chosen by the GM based on what the device must actually do.

The GM has full creative freedom to decide components, quantities, availability and origin: they can be purchased, salvaged from machinery, obtained as a reward or become the objective of a search during the adventure.

The indicative value given in the table serves only as an economic reference. It is not a shopping list or a mandatory cost. If the crew already owns some components, they do not have to pay for them again; if the design requires rare or unique materials, the GM can increase their value or require them to be obtained narratively.

A rare material does not automatically make the Module more powerful: mechanical values still depend on Tier, Function and any Special Effect.

10.3 · Commissioning the work

A Cyborg can entrust construction to a Mechanic or external workshop. The price in the External professional column normally includes labour, ordinary materials and installation.

Exceptional materials, unique components or technologies requiring a specific adventure are not automatically included in the price. A professional capable of building d12 or d20 Modules is not guaranteed merely by possessing Berry and remains the GM's choice.

When the work is commissioned, the player does not make the construction Check: the hired professional is assumed to have the required proficiency.

10.4 · Construction example

d8 Telescopic Arm

  • Technology Tier: d8.
  • Function: extendable arm within the normal d8 Reach.
  • Minimum Mechanics: d8.
  • Check: Cunning + Mechanics against Threshold 10.
  • Time: 16 hours of work.
  • Materials: chosen by the GM from those consistent with the design; indicative total value about 5,000 ฿.
  • External commission: about 15,000 ฿, including installation and ordinary materials.

The GM can freely decide which components from their Catalogue are needed. If the character salvages them during the adventure, they use them instead of purchasing them. A margin of +4 in construction normally uses the Masterpiece rules already provided by the Workshop.

10.5 · Installing, replacing and upgrading

Installing a Module requires a free slot, Mechanical Body at least equal to its Tier, the complete device and suitable tools.

TierBase installation time
d630 minutes
d81 hour
d102 hours
d124 hours
d208 hours

Replacing a Module takes the time for the new installation and includes removing the previous one. An operation cannot be performed simultaneously with a Rest.

Upgrading a Module to a higher Tier is a new design: the Cyborg must meet the new requirement, and the GM determines which components of the old device can be reused. Upgrading does not occur automatically when Mechanical Body increases.

10.6 · Module integrity and repair

A Module has two states: Intact and Damaged. Normal damage suffered by the Cyborg does not automatically damage installed Modules.

A Module can become Damaged when a rule targets it, a consequence directly affects the device, or the design suffers an Overload or specified malfunction.

A Damaged Module cannot use its Active function, Special Effect or linked Techniques until repaired.

Chapter 11 ·

The Cyborg's Body and Healing

Hit Points represent the Cyborg's overall integrity. Module Integrity is managed separately: a Cyborg can be severely wounded with all Modules intact, or be at full health with a Damaged Module.

11.2 · Field Repair

Outside combat, a Mechanic can directly repair a Cyborg's body. The operation requires Mechanic's tools, ordinary spare parts and 10 minutes of work.

Check: Cunning + Mechanics against Threshold 8.

Success: the Cyborg recovers 1 Mechanics Die of HP.

A Cyborg can benefit from only one Field Repair between two Short Rests. A Cyborg who personally has the Mechanic's skills can perform the operation on themselves.

11.3 · Flesh and Metal

d12 Talent · Passive

When performing Field Repair on a Cyborg, add +1 Mechanics Die to the HP restored. If the target also has a Damaged Module, during the same operation you can restore one Module to operation, provided the materials needed for its repair are available.

Flesh and Metal does not remove any special Module requirements or allow unique or destroyed components to be rebuilt for free.

11.4 · HP and Modules remain separate

Recovering HP does not change the state of Modules. Repairing a Module does not restore HP, unless an ability expressly states both.

Being a Cyborg does not automatically grant self-repair in combat. Immediate healing, instant repairs or additional recovery require a Technique, Talent, Special Effect or another ability allowing them.

11.5 · Stabilising a Cyborg

Normal Stabilisation through Medicine represents a biological operation and does not automatically apply to a Cyborg body.

An equivalent operation must use Mechanics or an ability expressly allowing work on a Cyborg.

Stabilising a Cyborg does not automatically restore HP or repair their Modules.

HP recovery still follows Field Repair and the Cyborg's other specific abilities.

Chapter 12 ·

Prosthetics, Gadgets and Non-Cyborg Characters

Not every mechanical object connected to a body is a Module.

12.1 · Ordinary prosthetics and gadgets

A simple prosthetic, worn tool or gadget can be ordinary equipment. In this case it does not occupy slots or use Activation, Special Effects or Technology Tiers from the Module system.

An ordinary object does not become a Module merely because it is attached to the body.

12.2 · When it becomes a true Module

A device is a Module when designed and recorded as such, occupying a slot and using this manual's rules. Only then can it gain Tier Functions, Special Effects and Technique links provided for Modules.

12.3 · Non-Cyborg characters

A non-Cyborg character can use normal prosthetics and gadgets as equipment. To install a true Module, they must possess an ability explicitly granting access to slots and the Module system.

Mere proficiency in Mechanics allows devices to be built or repaired, but does not automatically grant slots in your body.

12.4 · Legacy rules

Abilities from previous configurations, such as Modular Arsenal or Self-Repair, are not automatically part of normal V2 progression. If the GM decides to import them, they work according to their own sheets and do not establish precedents for creating more powerful new Modules.

Chapter 14 ·

Creating a Custom Module

Creating a Module does not require a new sub-rule. Use this procedure and stop when the sheet already clearly describes the device.

14.1 · Quick procedure

  1. Define the concept: what is the device and what is its Main Function?
  2. Choose the Tier, which cannot exceed Mechanical Body.
  3. Assign Category and Structure: Functional or Weapon Module.
  4. Take only the values needed for the Function from Chapter 4 and apply Chapter 5's rules.
  5. If needed, add only one Special Effect from Chapter 6.
  6. Decide whether it uses Energy or Charges and record consumption and recharging.
  7. Complete Limits, Compatible Techniques and the final sheet; then build and install it according to Chapter 10.

14.2 · Consistency check

Before approving the design, check four questions:

  • Is it doing one main thing, or does it contain multiple hidden Modules?
  • Do the values respect the chosen Tier?
  • Do damage, Statuses or Technique modifications come from a rule that actually grants them?
  • Is the Special Effect a single benefit, or does it conceal multiple independent advantages?

If the four answers are clear, the Module is normally ready to use.

14.3 · Example

Design: Mechanical Harpoon

  • Character's Mechanical Body: d10.
  • Chosen Tier: d10.
  • Category: Control · Mobility.
  • Structure: Functional.
  • Function: cable and harpoon with 10 m Reach and a 500 kg operating load; can anchor and drag compatible objects.
  • Special Effect: Unlock · Attraction.
  • Energy: None.
  • Compatible Techniques: Techniques executed through a cable or harpoon.

The Module can therefore attach, support or pull objects within its limits. Against a creature it does not automatically apply Attraction: to do so, it uses a compatible Technique benefiting from the Module's Unlock.

14.4 · Final rule

The Module system should expand the Cyborg's possibilities, not turn every device into its own ruleset. If a function is already covered by the tables, use the table; if an effect is already covered by the Builder, use the Builder.

Chapter 15 ·

Guide to Cyborg Builds

This chapter introduces no additional requirements. It is a guide to choosing Roles, Skills, Talents, Prestige and Haki when creating a Cyborg. A character can completely ignore these combinations and build their path in any other way allowed by the rules.

Being a Cyborg does not require choosing Engineer, Mechanics or a particular Haki. Modules can be built, purchased or commissioned: the Role describes what the character does, not what they must be to use their mechanical body.

15.1 · Before choosing the Role

Mechanical Body, Attributes, Skills and Roles grow separately. Mechanical Body determines Module slots and Technology Tier and ordinarily stops at d20; it does not enter Prestige. Attributes, Role Skills and Haki can instead continue beyond d20 according to the Prestige Manual.

For a Cyborg, the most natural pairings between Attribute Prestige and Role path are:

  • Strength + Crusher or physical Striker: Strength Prestige expands environmental destruction and Colossal Launch when the abilities used are compatible.
  • Technique + technical Striker or mobile Swordsman: Technique Prestige develops Lightning Displacement and Superhuman Agility in compatible paths.
  • Cunning + Sniper: Cunning Prestige develops effective range and Shot Geometry; Observation remains the Skill that unlocks Sniper Talents.
  • Spirit + Haki build: Spirit Prestige allows investment in Indomitable Will, Colour Attunement and Resurgence of Will.

Haki does not depend on Mechanical Body and is not necessary to be an effective Cyborg. When developed, however, it can complement some archetypes very well.

15.6 · Build examples

Cyborg Gunner

  • Key Role: Fighter · Sniper. The second Role is unrestricted; Engineer · Mechanic is particularly suitable if the character wants to personally maintain their systems.
  • Priorities: Cunning, Observation and the Attribute used by the Weapon Module.
  • Modules: integrated cannon or rifle, sensor, thruster or positioning system.
  • Talents: Long Shot, Targeted Shot, Anticipated Shot and other Sniper abilities compatible with the weapon used.
  • Prestige: Cunning develops range and trajectory control; Observation grants access to Sniper Prestige Talents. If the character also chooses Mechanic, Mechanics Prestige can develop the abilities of that path.
  • Haki: Observation is the most natural choice; Armament remains valid for those wanting to increase their attacks' effectiveness.

Juggernaut

  • Roles: Fighter · Crusher + Engineer · Mechanic.
  • Priorities: Strength, Athletics and Mechanics.
  • Modules: compatible integrated blunt weapon, Armour, anchoring and brief thruster.
  • Talents: Heavy Strike, Planted Feet / Absolute Centre of Gravity, Rapid Response and Flesh and Metal.
  • Prestige: Strength for destruction and launching; Athletics for Unstoppable Maul, Seismic Wave or Colossus Stop. Prodigious Reconstruction is excellent if Mechanics also enters Prestige.
  • Haki: Armament; Spirit becomes interesting if the build invests heavily in Haki upkeep.

Integrated Blade

  • Roles: Fighter · Swordsman. The second Role is unrestricted; Engineer · Mechanic is a suitable choice if the character wants to personally maintain their Modules.
  • Priorities: Athletics and the Attribute chosen for blade Techniques. Technique favours mobility; Strength favours a more destructive approach.
  • Modules: compatible integrated blade, thruster, shield or sensor.
  • Prestige: Athletics unlocks Sovereign Slash, Colossal Cut, Impassable Guard and Absolute Blade Mastery; Technique in Prestige adds the mobility package when compatible.
  • Haki: Armament is the most immediate choice; Observation is excellent for a more defensive and reactive variant.

Mobile Assaulter

  • Roles: Fighter · Striker + an unrestricted second Role; Engineer is useful but not necessary.
  • Priorities: Strength + Athletics for impact or Technique + Acrobatics for mobility and precision.
  • Modules: thrusters, armour, sensors, cables and utility systems. Avoid basing Striker Techniques on a Weapon Module: the Style remains unarmed.
  • Prestige: Strength leads towards Titan’s Power and Titanic Wave; Technique develops mobility and Absolute Precision. Endless Flurry is common to both paths.
  • Haki: Armament for the Strength path; Observation for the Technique path. Both are valid if the character develops sufficient Spirit.

Living Workshop

  • Key Role: Engineer · Mechanic. The second Role can be freely chosen based on the desired function in the crew.
  • Priorities: Cunning + Mechanics; high Mechanical Body if the character wants to use many different Modules.
  • Modules: integrated tools, sensors, repair systems, defences and a single emergency offensive Module.
  • Crafting: the character can normally build and repair devices using Mechanics and other appropriate Skills. Special designs can be obtained during the campaign and built according to Workshop rules.
  • Prestige: Prodigious Reconstruction is particularly suitable for keeping themselves and other Cyborgs operational; Mechanical Direction and Machine Dominion instead favour managing systems.
  • Haki: entirely optional. A technology-oriented Cyborg can mainly invest in their Skills, Talents and Modules.

Mechanised Commander

  • Roles: Captain + a technological or combat path of your choice.
  • Priorities: Spirit, Communication and the statistics needed for the second Role.
  • Modules: sensors, communication, barrier, field control and mobility.
  • Prestige: Communication develops the Captain's catalogue; Spirit in Prestige is particularly effective if the character possesses multiple Haki Colours.
  • Haki: Observation to read the field; Conqueror’s only if granted by the story and GM.

15.7 · Final advice

First choose what you want to do during a scene; then choose Modules, Role and progressions that make it more interesting. Do not build the character around the idea that a Cyborg must have Mechanics, five weapons or every possible system.

A successful build tends to have two or three strong ideas: for example, mobility + precision, armour + control, or design + support. Slots are limited precisely to encourage the player to give their technology an identity. The rest can be entrusted to the crew, ordinary equipment or a professional.
